=== injured
title: Someone is injured
summary: Step by step through danger, bleeding, response, breathing and injuries, then keeping them warm and getting help.
icon: heart-pulse
---
@ start
**Stop and look before you go in.** Traffic, water, falling rock, edges, fire, electricity, livestock. Don't become a second casualty.
? Is it safe for you to reach them?
+ Yes, it's safe: cat-bleed
+ No, it isn't safe: not-safe

@ not-safe
**Don't go in.** Shout to them and talk them towards you if they can move.
- In water: **reach** with a stick or clothing, or **throw** something that floats. Don't swim in after them.
- Rockfall, traffic or fire: wait until it stops, or make it safe (warn traffic, put out the fire) without taking risks.
- Can't reach them at all: signal for help now.
? Has it become safe to reach them?
+ Yes, I can reach them now: cat-bleed
> [[a:water-rescue]] [[a:signalling-rescue]]

@ cat-bleed
As you reach them, look for **blood spurting, pouring or pooling fast**. This kills in minutes, so it comes before everything else, even the airway.
? Is there bleeding like that?
+ Yes: bleed-control
+ No: response

@ bleed-control
1. **Press hard** on the wound with a pad, cloth or your hand. Don't let go.
2. Deep wound (thigh, groin, armpit, neck): **pack** cloth tightly into it, then press with both hands.
3. Arm or leg, and pressure isn't stopping it: **tourniquet** 5–7 cm above the wound, not on a joint. Tighten until the bleeding stops. **Note the time.** Never loosen it.
4. Still bleeding? Add a second tourniquet above the first, or another pad on top and press harder.

Shout for help now if anyone is near.
? Is the bleeding controlled, or someone else pressing on it?
+ Yes, move on: response
> [[a:severe-bleeding]]

@ response
Kneel beside them. Tap their shoulders and say loudly: "**Can you hear me? Open your eyes.**"
? Do they respond?
+ Yes, they answer or move: breathing
+ No response: airway

@ airway
**Open the airway:** one hand on the forehead, two fingers under the chin, tilt the head back.

**Check breathing for up to 10 seconds:** look at the chest, listen and feel for breath on your cheek. Occasional gasps are **not** normal breathing.
? Are they breathing normally?
+ Yes, breathing normally: recovery
+ No, or only gasping: cpr
> [[a:primary-survey]]

@ cpr
**Start CPR now.** Shout for help; send someone for help straight away if anyone is with you.
- Heel of hand, centre of the chest, other hand on top, arms straight.
- Push **5–6 cm** deep, **100–120 a minute**. Use the metronome.
- **30 compressions, then 2 rescue breaths.** Not trained or willing to give breaths? Do non-stop compressions.
- **Child**, or after **drowning** or in **hypothermia**: give **5 rescue breaths first**. Child: push one third of the chest depth (about 5 cm).
? Do they wake, move and start breathing normally?
+ Yes, breathing normally: recovery
+ No change: cpr-continue
> [[t:cpr]] [[a:cpr-adult]] [[a:cpr-child]]

@ cpr-continue
**Keep going.** Don't stop to check unless they wake and breathe normally.
- Swap with someone every 2 minutes if you can, with as little pause as possible.
- Stop only if they breathe normally, help takes over, you are too exhausted to go on, or it becomes unsafe.
- **Alone, adult casualty, and people or a road are only minutes away:** go and raise the alarm first, then come back and carry on CPR.
- **Alone with a child:** do 1 minute of CPR first, then go for help.
- **Hypothermia:** keep going for as long as you can. Nobody is dead until they are warm and dead.

Note the time you started in the casualty log.
> [[t:cpr]] [[t:casualty]] [[a:cpr-adult]] [[a:cpr-child]] [[a:when-to-get-help]]

@ recovery
**Recovery position** keeps the airway open:
1. Insulate the ground first if you can (mat, rucksack, bracken).
2. Near arm out at a right angle; far hand against their near cheek.
3. Bend the far knee and pull it towards you to roll them onto their side. Tilt the head back.

**Neck or back injury possible** (a fall, a heavy blow) and breathing fine on their back? Keep them still with the head in line instead. Roll them, keeping the head in line, only if they vomit or you must leave them.

**Send someone for help now.** Keep checking their breathing.
? What next?
+ Breathing steadily, carry on checking them: bleed-other
+ They stop breathing normally: cpr
> [[a:recovery-position]]

@ breathing
They can respond. Now look at their breathing.
? Are they choking or struggling to breathe?
+ Choking (can't speak, cough or breathe): choking
+ Wheezing, very breathless or chest pain: breathing-hard
+ Breathing comfortably: bleed-other

@ choking
- If they can cough, **encourage coughing**.
- If they can't: lean them forward and give up to **5 back blows** between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
- Then up to **5 abdominal thrusts**: fist above the belly button, pull sharply **in and up**. Not for babies.
- Repeat back blows and thrusts until it clears. Anyone given abdominal thrusts needs a doctor afterwards.
? What happened?
+ It cleared and they're breathing: bleed-other
+ They became unresponsive: cpr
> [[a:choking]]

@ breathing-hard
- **Sit them upright**, in whatever position helps. Keep them calm; loosen tight clothing.
- **Asthma:** help them use their reliever inhaler (usually blue), one puff every 30–60 seconds, up to 10 puffs.
- **Swollen lips, tongue or throat, wheeze, rash after a sting or food:** possible anaphylaxis. Use their adrenaline auto-injector in the outer thigh at once; a second dose after 5 minutes if no better.
- **Chest pain** spreading to the arm, jaw or back: keep them at rest, half sitting.
- **Send someone for help now** for any of these.
? Settled as much as you can?
+ Yes, carry on: bleed-other
> [[a:asthma-attack]] [[a:stings-anaphylaxis]] [[a:heart-attack-stroke]]

@ bleed-other
**Check head to toe** for bleeding you missed: under clothing, under the body, in the hair. Press firmly on any bleeding wound for **10 minutes** without peeking, then bandage.

Then look for **shock**: pale, grey, cold and clammy skin; fast weak pulse; fast breathing; thirst, dizziness, confusion.
? Are there signs of shock?
+ Yes: shock
+ No: head-neck
> [[a:severe-bleeding]] [[a:wound-care]]

@ shock
- Keep bleeding controlled; cool any burns.
- **Lie them down.** Raise and support the legs, unless you suspect a leg, pelvis or spine injury.
- **Insulate underneath** and cover them.
- **Nothing to eat or drink**; moisten the lips only.
- **Send someone for help.** Check breathing and response every few minutes.
? Done what you can?
+ Yes, carry on: head-neck
> [[a:shock]]

@ head-neck
? Did they fall, take a blow to the head, or have neck or back pain, tingling or weakness?
+ Yes: spine
+ No: limbs

@ spine
- **Keep the head and neck still** and in line with the body. Kneel at the head and hold it if you can.
- Don't move them unless they are in danger or you must, to protect the airway.
- **Send someone for help** if they were knocked out, are confused, drowsy or vomiting, have a seizure, fluid from the nose or ears, or numbness or weakness.
- After a head injury, **never leave them alone**, and watch them closely for 24 hours.
? Carry on?
+ Yes: limbs
> [[a:head-injury]]

@ limbs
? Any other injuries?
+ A broken bone, bad sprain or dislocation: fracture
+ A burn: burn
+ No, or all dealt with: warm

@ fracture
- **Don't move** the injured part. Support it in the position you found it.
- Bone through the skin: cover with a clean pad and press **around** it, not on it.
- **Splint** it with padding to the body or a rigid object, including the joints above and below.
- Check fingers or toes beyond it (colour, warmth, feeling) **every 15 minutes**.
- Suspected broken thigh or pelvis: keep them still and send for help.
? Anything else?
+ There's a burn too: burn
+ No, carry on: warm
> [[a:fractures-splinting]] [[a:sprains-strains]]

@ burn
- **Cool it with cool or lukewarm water for 20 minutes.** Pour clean water from a bottle if there's no stream. No ice.
- Remove jewellery and clothing near the burn, unless stuck to it.
- **Cover loosely** with cling film, a clean plastic bag or a clean non-fluffy cloth.
- Keep the rest of them **warm**: cooling a big burn can bring on hypothermia.
- Send for help for large or deep burns, or burns to the face, hands, feet, groin or airway.
? Carry on?
+ Yes: warm
> [[a:burns]]

@ warm
Injured people get cold fast, even in summer.
- **Insulate underneath first**: mat, rucksack, rope, bracken. The ground steals more heat than the air.
- Get out of the wind and rain: tarp, survival bag, the lee of a wall or boulder.
- Dry layers on, wet off; **hat** on. Wrap them in a survival bag.
- Warm sweet drinks **only if fully alert** and not in shock or badly hurt.
? Are they shivering hard, clumsy, confused or drowsy?
+ Yes: hypo
+ No: record
> [[a:insulation-ground]]

@ hypo
They may be **hypothermic**.
- Stop all heat loss: shelter, insulation, dry layers, survival bag.
- **Warm the core**, not the limbs: share body heat inside a bag, or warm (not hot) bottles wrapped in cloth in the armpits.
- **Confused, drowsy or no longer shivering:** severe. Handle very gently, keep them lying flat, nothing to drink, and **send for help**.
- Don't rub limbs or apply direct heat.
? Carry on?
+ Yes: record
> [[a:hypothermia]]

@ record
**Write it down.** Open the casualty log and record:
- The time, and what happened.
- Injuries, and what you did (tourniquet time, CPR start).
- Every **10–15 minutes**: response (Alert, Voice, Pain, Unresponsive), breathing rate, pulse, skin colour.

Rescuers and whoever reaches a phone will need this. Note any change for better or worse.
? Done?
+ Yes: help
> [[t:casualty]] [[a:location-report]]

@ help
**They need rescue** if they were unresponsive at any point, needed CPR, had severe bleeding, a suspected spine, thigh or pelvis injury, a head injury with confusion, hypothermia, breathing trouble, or can't walk.
? Can they walk safely, with help, to a road or people?
+ Yes, minor injury and they can walk: walk-out
+ No, they need rescue: who-goes

@ walk-out
**Walk out together, slowly.**
- Take the easiest, safest route to the nearest path, road or house, not the shortest.
- Carry their kit. Stop often; keep them warm, fed and drinking.
- Turn back or stop and shelter if they get worse, or if daylight is running out.
- When you reach people, get them checked by a doctor.
> [[a:moving-with-injury]] [[a:finding-help]] [[a:emergency-night-out]]

@ who-goes
This phone can't call for help. Help comes from a messenger, from signalling, or from passers-by.
? Who else is with you?
+ Two or more others: send-two
+ One other person: one-other
+ Nobody, just me and the casualty: alone-decide

@ send-two
**Send two people together** for help. Never one alone.
1. Write a **location report**: grid reference from the GPS tool, a description, what happened, when, who is hurt and how they are now.
2. They take the safest route to the nearest road, farm, village or busy path, and get anyone with a phone to raise the alarm (999 or 112, ask for Police then Mountain Rescue, or Coastguard on the coast).
3. Everyone else **stays with the casualty**: sheltered, warm, visible, signalling at intervals.
> [[a:location-report]] [[t:gps]] [[a:finding-help]] [[a:when-you-reach-help]] [[a:signalling-rescue]]

@ one-other
**Signal first.** Six whistle blasts or torch flashes a minute, then a minute's rest, for 10–15 minutes. Someone may be close.

If no one answers:
- One of you **stays** with the casualty; the other **goes for help** with a written location report and grid reference.
- The one going takes the safest route they know, in daylight if possible, and takes no risks.
- The one staying keeps the casualty warm, keeps the log and keeps signalling.
> [[a:signalling-rescue]] [[a:location-report]] [[a:finding-help]] [[a:cant-be-moved]]

@ alone-decide
**Signal first**: six blasts or flashes a minute, then a minute's rest. Do it for 10–15 minutes before deciding anything.
? Nobody answering. Can you leave them safely?
+ No, they need me here (CPR, bleeding, airway, can't be left): stay-signal
+ Yes, they're stable and help is within reach: leaving

@ stay-signal
**Stay with them.**
- Shelter, insulate and keep them warm. Keep checking breathing and the log.
- **Signal at intervals**, and every time you hear or see anyone, a vehicle or an aircraft.
- Make the spot visible: bright kit spread out, a big **X** on open ground (need medical help).
- Turn the phone off between uses to save battery.
> [[a:cant-be-moved]] [[a:signalling-rescue]] [[t:signal]] [[a:be-visible]]

@ leaving
**Before you go:**
1. Put them in the recovery position if they are drowsy, insulated and wrapped up, with water and food in reach if they are alert.
2. Save a **waypoint** and write the grid reference. Mark the spot so it can be found (bright kit, cairn, an arrow).
3. Leave a note with them: what happened, time you left, where you are going.
4. Go by the safest route to the nearest people or road, and get anyone with a phone to raise the alarm. Tell them where the casualty is.
