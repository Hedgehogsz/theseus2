=== dark-cold
title: It's getting dark and cold
summary: Decide whether you can reach shelter before dark or must stop now, then make camp, keep warm, signal and get through the night.
icon: sunset
---
@ start
Open the sun tool to see how long until sunset. Under cloud or trees it gets too dark to move safely sooner than you think.

**Rule:** stop and make camp with **at least an hour of light left**. Everything takes three times as long in the dark.
? Is anyone wet through, or already shivering?
+ Yes: wet
+ No, we're fairly dry and warm: reach
> [[t:sun]]

@ wet
**Wet, wind and dark** is how people die of cold in Britain, even well above freezing.
- Change wet base layers for dry ones, one item at a time, out of the wind.
- No dry spares: wring clothes out, put them back on, and put a windproof or waterproof on top. Wool and fleece still keep some warmth when wet.
- **Hat on.** Eat something sugary now.
? Is anyone stumbling, confused, slurring, drowsy, or has stopped shivering?
+ Yes: hypo-now
+ No: reach
> [[a:cold-and-wet]]

@ hypo-now
**Stop right here and treat them.** Don't push on for shelter.
- Out of the wind, **insulate underneath**, wrap them in dry layers and a survival bag.
- Share body heat. Warm sweet drinks only if they are fully alert.
- Drowsy or no longer shivering: severe. Handle gently, keep them lying flat, and **send someone for help** if two can go safely.
? Done what you can?
+ Yes, now make camp here: stop-now
> [[a:hypothermia]]

@ reach
? Is there a road, house, bothy or other shelter you can reach well before dark, on a route you know is safe?
+ Yes: go-now
+ No, or I'm not sure: stop-now
> [[a:bothies]] [[t:map]]

@ go-now
**Go now, steadily, and together.**
- Set a **turn-back time**: if you're not there with an hour of light left, stop and camp.
- Get the head torch out and to hand now.
- Don't take shortcuts across unknown ground in failing light.
? How did it go?
+ We got there: arrived
+ Light is running out first: stop-now
> [[a:night-navigation]] [[a:naismith-pacing]]

@ arrived
**You're in.** Now get warm and dry:
- Wet layers off, dry layers on. Hang wet clothes to dry, or put them in a bag.
- Insulate underneath: a stone floor steals heat.
- Eat and drink something warm if you can. A fire only in a proper fireplace, and never a stove or charcoal in a closed room (carbon monoxide).
- In a bothy, write your name, route and plans in the bothy book.
> [[a:sleeping-warm]] [[a:bothies]] [[a:drying-clothes]]

@ stop-now
**Stop and camp now.** Pick your spot:
- **Out of the wind:** the lee of a wall, boulder, bank or dense conifers.
- **Off wet ground**, not in a hollow that floods or beside a stream that could rise.
- Not under dead branches, not near crag edges.
? What shelter have you got?
+ A tarp or tent: pitch
+ A survival bag only: bag
+ Nothing: natural
> [[a:site-selection]]

@ pitch
- Pitch it **low**, with the back or a closed end to the wind.
- Peg or weight every edge; a flapping tarp loses heat and sleep.
- Keep everything you need inside with you.
? Pitched?
+ Yes: insulate
> [[a:tarp-a-frame]] [[a:tarp-rigging]]

@ bag
- Get in feet first with **all your layers on**, and pull it up to your chest or over your head, leaving your face out.
- Sit or lie on your rucksack inside or under the bag.
- It will get damp inside from condensation. That's normal: it still keeps the wind off.
? In?
+ Yes: insulate

@ natural
No time for a proper shelter: a debris hut takes hours. Instead:
- Get tight into the best windbreak you can find: a wall, boulder, bank, fallen tree or dense conifers.
- Pile up dead bracken, leaves or heather to sit in and pull over your legs.
- Huddle together if there are several of you.
? Settled in?
+ Yes: insulate
> [[a:shelter-priorities]]

@ insulate
**The ground steals heat faster than the air.**
- Make a layer **15–30 cm thick** of dry bracken, heather, leaves or conifer boughs, or sit on your rucksack, rope and spare clothing.
- Press down with your fist: if you can feel the ground, add more.
- **Every layer on**, hat first. Dry socks on.
? Is a fire possible and safe here?
+ Yes, dry fuel and a safe spot: fire
+ No: no-fire
> [[a:insulation-ground]] [[a:layering]]

@ fire
- **Gather all your wood before dark**, three times more than you think, from matchstick to wrist-thick.
- Build it in front of your shelter, with a rock or log wall behind it to reflect heat.
- Never on peat or dry moorland, and never inside an enclosed shelter.
- Get a pot of water on: hot drinks, and a hot water bottle for the night.
? Fire going?
+ Yes: eat-drink
+ Couldn't get it lit: no-fire
> [[a:fire-essentials]] [[a:wet-weather-fire]] [[a:fire-reflector]] [[a:fire-safety]]

@ no-fire
You can get through the night without a fire.
- More insulation underneath, more layers on top, survival bag round you.
- Huddle together, back to back, the coldest person in the middle.
- Warm your hands in your armpits; keep feet dry.
? Ready?
+ Yes: eat-drink

@ eat-drink
**Eat and drink now**, and again before you sleep.
- Your body makes heat from food. Fatty and sugary food helps most.
- Cold hides thirst and makes you pee more: drink anyway. Hot drinks if you have a fire.
- Pee before you settle down: a full bladder costs heat.
? Could anyone be out looking for you tonight?
+ Yes, or maybe: signal
+ No: overnight
> [[a:food-priorities]] [[f:water-now]]

@ signal
**Signal with light.** Searchers often work through the night.
- **Six torch flashes in a minute**, then a minute's rest. Repeat for a few minutes at a time.
- Signal in bursts every 10–15 minutes to save battery, and **every time** you see a light or hear a voice, an engine or a helicopter.
- Six whistle blasts carry further than a shout. The reply is three.
- A fire is a beacon too.
? Ready to settle for the night?
+ Yes: overnight
> [[t:signal]] [[t:torch]] [[a:signalling-rescue]]

@ overnight
**Through the night:**
- Huddle; keep moving your toes and fingers. Clench and relax big muscles when you start shivering.
- A bottle of hot (not boiling) water, cap tight, wrapped in a sock, inside your clothes or bag.
- Keep tomorrow's clothes and the phone inside your bag so they stay warm and dry.
- Wake each other every hour or two to check on everyone.
? Is anyone stumbling, confused, slurring, drowsy, or has stopped shivering?
+ Yes: hypo-night
+ No: morning
> [[a:emergency-night-out]] [[a:sleeping-warm]]

@ hypo-night
**Treat hypothermia now.**
- More insulation underneath, all dry layers on, survival bag round them.
- **Warm the core:** body-to-body inside a bag, warm bottles wrapped in cloth in the armpits and on the chest.
- Warm sweet drinks and food only if fully alert.
- **Severe** (drowsy, not shivering): handle very gently, keep them flat, check breathing for a full minute. Send two people for help at first light, or sooner if they can go safely. No breathing: start CPR.
- Don't rub limbs; no alcohol; no direct heat.
> [[a:hypothermia]] [[f:injured]] [[a:cpr-adult]]

@ morning
**At first light:** warm up, eat, drink and dry what you can. Then decide whether to stay and signal or walk out.
> [[f:lost]] [[a:stay-or-go]] [[a:signalling-rescue]]
