=== lost
title: I'm lost
summary: Stop and think, deal with injuries and the cold, work out where you are, then decide whether to stay and signal or walk out.
icon: map-pin-off
---
@ start
**STOP.** Don't keep walking in the hope it will come right.
- **Stop:** sit down, put on a layer, eat and drink something.
- **Think:** where were you last sure of your position? How long ago? Which way have you been going?
- **Observe:** big features around you: hills, valleys, rivers, forest edges, pylons, roads.
- **Plan:** choose one sensible option and stick to it. This guide will help.
? Is anyone injured, very cold or exhausted?
+ Yes: hurt
+ No: light
> [[a:stop-method]] [[a:lost-person-actions]]

@ hurt
**Deal with the person first**, then plan to **stay put** and bring help to you. Moving an injured or exhausted person in unknown ground is how accidents get worse.
? Can two people go for help together, and leave someone with the casualty?
+ Yes: send-two
+ No, I'm alone or there aren't enough of us: stay-signal
> [[f:injured]] [[a:hypothermia]]

@ send-two
**Two go together**, the rest stay with the casualty.
1. Work out the grid reference with the GPS tool if you can, and write a **location report**: where, what happened, who is hurt, what kit you have.
2. The two take the safest route they know to a path, road, farm or village, in daylight if possible, and get anyone with a phone to raise the alarm.
3. Those staying shelter, keep warm and signal at intervals.
> [[a:location-report]] [[t:gps]] [[a:finding-help]] [[a:when-you-reach-help]]

@ light
Open the sun tool and check how much daylight is left. Look at the weather too.
? How long have you got, and what's the weather doing?
+ Under about an hour of light, or fog, storm or heavy rain: shelter-now
+ Plenty of daylight and the weather is reasonable: where
> [[t:sun]]

@ shelter-now
**Shelter first; navigation can wait.** Walking off in the dark or in fog is how people fall, get hurt or go further astray.
- Get out of the wind: the lee of a wall, boulder, bank or dense trees.
- Sit on your rucksack or a thick pile of bracken, heather or leaves.
- **Every layer on**, hat first. Get into a survival bag if you have one.

Once you are sheltered, try to work out where you are.
? Sheltered?
+ Yes: where
> [[a:emergency-night-out]] [[f:dark-cold]]

@ where
Switch the phone on and open the GPS tool. Stand in the open, away from crags and dense trees, and give it a few minutes to get a fix.
? Has it given you a grid reference?
+ Yes: located
+ No fix, or the phone is flat: relocate
> [[t:gps]] [[t:map]]

@ relocate
**Without GPS:** use the map and compass if you have them.
- Find **one feature you can identify for certain** (a lake, summit, stream junction, wall corner, pylon line) and match the shape of the land to the contours.
- Set the map to north with the compass, and line the map up with what you see.
- Think back along your route: time walked and direction give you a rough area.
? Do you now know where you are?
+ Yes: located
+ No, but I can retrace my steps safely to where I was sure: retrace
+ No: told
> [[a:relocating]] [[a:map-compass-basics]] [[a:finding-north]]

@ retrace
**Go back the way you came**, only if the way is obvious and safe, and you have the daylight. Stop as soon as you recognise where you are, or if it gets dark or the ground gets dangerous.
? Did you get back to somewhere you know?
+ Yes: located
+ No: told

@ located
**You're no longer lost.** Write the grid reference down. On the map, find:
- The nearest path, track, road or building.
- The **safest** way there, not the shortest. Look for crags, steep ground, rivers and bogs in between.
- How long it will take: about 1 hour per 5 km, plus 10 minutes for every 100 m of climb, more on rough ground.
? Is there a safe route you can finish in the daylight and weather you have?
+ Yes: walk-known
+ No: told
> [[t:map]] [[a:naismith-pacing]]

@ walk-known
**Go, steadily.**
- Check your position every 15–30 minutes. Turn the phone off between checks to save battery.
- Use handrails: paths, walls, streams and fences that run your way.
- Set a **turn-back time**. If you're not there with an hour of light left, stop and shelter.
- If the route gets harder than the map suggested, go back to your last known point.
> [[a:handrails-catching]] [[t:map]] [[a:emergency-night-out]]

@ told
? Does someone know your route and when you're due back?
+ Yes, they will raise the alarm: stay-signal
+ No, or I'm not sure: can-go

@ can-go
Walking out is only for when nobody will come looking, and you are in a fit state to do it safely.
? Are you fit and warm, with daylight and reasonable weather?
+ Yes: walk-out
+ No: stay-signal
> [[a:stay-or-go]]

@ stay-signal
**Stay put and get found.** Moving makes you harder to find, and most accidents happen while trying to get down.
- **Shelter** out of the wind, insulate underneath, all layers on.
- **Signal:** six whistle blasts or torch flashes in a minute, then a minute's rest. Repeat at intervals, and every time you see or hear anyone.
- **Be visible:** bright kit spread out on open ground; a big **V** (need help) or **X** (need medical help).
- Turn the phone off between uses; check the GPS once in a while so you can give your position.
- Reassess in daylight.
> [[a:stay-or-go]] [[a:signalling-rescue]] [[t:signal]] [[a:be-visible]] [[a:emergency-night-out]]

@ walk-out
**Walk out downhill**, towards valleys, farms and roads. Leave a note or an arrow where you were, in case searchers come.
? What can you see nearby?
+ A path, track, wall, fence or power line: follow-linear
+ Nothing like that, but there's a stream or a valley: follow-water

@ follow-linear
**Follow it**, downhill when in doubt. Paths and tracks lead to car parks, farms and roads; walls and fences lead down to farmland; power lines lead to roads and houses.
- Keep to it; don't take shortcuts across unknown ground.
- Stop and shelter with an hour of light left.
- When you reach people, ask them to raise the alarm if anyone is still out there.
> [[a:linear-features]] [[a:finding-help]] [[a:pacing-yourself]]

@ follow-water
**Follow the water downhill**, walking beside it, not in it. Valleys in Britain almost always have a farm, a road or houses within a few kilometres.
- **Never** follow a stream into a gorge or over a waterfall: climb away and descend on the open slope beside it.
- Don't cross rivers in spate.
- Stop and shelter with an hour of light left.
> [[a:follow-water-downhill]] [[a:river-crossing]] [[a:finding-help]]
