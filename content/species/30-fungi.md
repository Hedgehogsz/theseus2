# UK fungi. Every fungus page also shows the app's "never eat wild fungi unless certain" banner.

=== death-cap
name: Death cap
latin: Amanita phalloides
group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: deadly
season: Jul–Nov (mainly Aug–Oct)
habitat: Broadleaf woodland, especially under oak and beech; also parks with those trees; mainly southern Britain
summary: Olive-green to yellowish cap, white gills, a skirt-like ring and a bag-like volva at the base. Causes most of the world's mushroom deaths.
lookalikes: field-mushroom, destroying-angel, horse-mushroom, common-puffball, giant-puffball
tags: amanita, amatoxin, alpha-amanitin, volva, deadly, green cap
wiki: Amanita phalloides
---
!danger Deadly: half a cap can kill an adult
Contains amatoxins, which destroy the liver and kidneys. Cooking, drying or freezing does not destroy them. Symptoms start **6–24 hours** after eating: violent vomiting and watery diarrhoea. A **false recovery** follows after about a day, then liver failure 3–5 days later. Anyone who may have eaten it needs hospital **immediately**, even if they feel fine. Get emergency help and keep a sample: [[a:poisoning]].
!end

## Identification
- Cap 5–15 cm, **olive-green, yellowish-green or bronze** (sometimes pale, nearly white), smooth, with faint radiating fibres, and usually no warts.
- Gills **white**, free (not touching the stem), crowded. Spore print **white**.
- Stem white, often with faint greenish zig-zag bands, with a drooping white **ring (skirt)** near the top.
- Base of the stem sits in a **white, bag-like volva (sac)**, often buried in leaf litter. **Always dig up the whole base.**
- Young specimens are white **"eggs"** that look like small puffballs. Cut them in half and you will see the outline of a cap and gills.
- Smell faintly sweet, becoming sickly with age.

## Lookalikes
- Edible mushrooms ([[s:field-mushroom]]) have pink-to-brown gills, no volva, and grow in grassland rather than under oak and beech. See [[a:compare-field-mushroom]].

=== destroying-angel
name: Destroying angel
latin: Amanita virosa
group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: deadly
season: Aug–Nov
habitat: Woodland on acid soils, under birch, pine and beech; commonest in Scotland and northern England
summary: Pure white mushroom with a ring, a shaggy stem and a sac-like volva. As deadly as the death cap.
lookalikes: field-mushroom, horse-mushroom, death-cap, st-georges-mushroom, giant-puffball
tags: amanita, amatoxin, white mushroom, volva, deadly
wiki: Amanita virosa
---
!danger Deadly
Same amatoxins and delayed symptoms as the [[s:death-cap]]. **Never eat any all-white gilled mushroom from woodland.** Get emergency help: [[a:poisoning]].
!end

## Identification
- Entirely **white**: cap 5–12 cm, often conical to bell-shaped and slightly lopsided, sticky when wet.
- Gills white, free; spore print white.
- Stem white, with a **shaggy, scaly** surface below a fragile ring.
- White, bag-like **volva** at the base, often hidden underground.

## Lookalikes
- [[s:field-mushroom]] and [[s:horse-mushroom]]: gills pink to brown (never pure white in a mature cap), no volva. See [[a:compare-field-mushroom]].

=== fly-agaric
name: Fly agaric
latin: Amanita muscaria
group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: poisonous, psychoactive
season: Aug–Nov
habitat: Under birch and pine (sometimes spruce), on heaths and in woods
summary: The fairy-tale toadstool: scarlet cap with white warty flecks, white gills and a ring. Poisonous and hallucinogenic; a close relative of the deadly death cap.
lookalikes: panther-cap, the-blusher, death-cap
tags: amanita, red toadstool, muscimol, ibotenic acid, magic mushroom, hallucinogen
wiki: Amanita muscaria
---
!danger An Amanita: same family as the death cap
The fly agaric is an **Amanita**, the family that includes the deadly [[s:death-cap]] and [[s:destroying-angel]], and the more poisonous [[s:panther-cap]]. Old or rain-washed caps lose their warts and fade to orange or yellow, and young "eggs" look like other Amanitas. **Never eat any Amanita.** See [[a:compare-amanitas]].
!end

## Identification
- Cap 8–20 cm, **bright red to orange** with scattered **white warts** (which rain can wash off); fine grooves at the edge with age.
- Gills white, free. Spore print white.
- Stem white with a hanging ring and a bulbous base ringed by **warty collars** (not a loose bag-like volva).
- Flesh white, yellowish just under the cap skin.
- Almost always with birch or pine; often in rings around a tree.

## Lookalikes
- **Deadly:** a faded fly agaric without warts can be confused with other Amanitas; the [[s:death-cap]] has an olive-green to yellowish cap, a skirt-like ring and a **bag-like volva**.
- [[s:panther-cap]]: brown cap, pure white warts; more toxic.
- [[s:the-blusher]]: pinkish-brown cap, flesh turns pink when damaged.

## Dangers
- Contains **ibotenic acid and muscimol**: confusion, agitation, hallucinations, drowsiness, vomiting, muscle twitching, and seizures in larger amounts. Rarely fatal in adults but dangerous, especially for **children and dogs**.
- The amount of toxin varies widely from mushroom to mushroom.
- **Impairment outdoors kills**: deep drowsiness alternating with agitation, poor coordination and confusion lead to falls, wandering, drowning and hypothermia.

## Law
- *Amanita muscaria* is **not** a controlled drug under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971. However, the **Psychoactive Substances Act 2016** makes it an offence to produce, supply or offer to supply any psychoactive substance for people to consume (up to 7 years in prison). Shops can no longer legally sell it for eating.

## If someone has eaten it
**Symptoms:** usually start **30 minutes–2 hours** after eating (up to 3 hours) and last **6–24 hours**: dizziness, vomiting, unsteadiness, confusion, alternating **deep sleepiness and agitation**, hallucinations, muscle jerks; fits and coma in severe cases, especially in children.

!danger Think "death cap" if
Symptoms start **6 hours or more** after eating, with **severe vomiting and watery diarrhoea**: a deadly Amanita may have been eaten by mistake. They need hospital **urgently, even if they seem to recover**.
!end

**First aid:**
- **Don't** make them vomit. Sips of water only.
- **Keep a sample** (with the base) and a photo; note what, how much and when.
- Keep them **safe**: away from water, edges and fire; stay with them. Keep them **warm**: a drowsy person cools fast ([[a:hypothermia]]).
- Drowsy or unresponsive: [[a:recovery-position]]; check breathing. Fits: [[a:seizures]]. Not breathing: CPR ([[t:cpr]]).
- **Get them to medical help**, or send someone. When you reach a phone or a person, ask for an ambulance and say which mushroom. See [[a:poisoning]].

=== panther-cap
name: Panther cap
latin: Amanita pantherina
group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: poisonous, psychoactive
season: Jul–Oct
habitat: Broadleaf woods (beech, oak) and sometimes conifers, especially on chalk and limestone
summary: Brown cap with pure white warts, white gills and a ring with a gutter-like rim to the volva. More toxic than fly agaric and can kill; a close relative of the death cap.
lookalikes: the-blusher, death-cap, fly-agaric
tags: amanita, brown cap, white warts, muscimol, ibotenic acid, hallucinogen
wiki: Amanita pantherina
---
!danger Can kill: an Amanita, like the death cap
The panther cap has more ibotenic acid and muscimol than the fly agaric and **has caused deaths**. It is an **Amanita**, in the same family as the deadly [[s:death-cap]] and [[s:destroying-angel]], and grows in the same woods. **Never eat any Amanita.** See [[a:compare-amanitas]].
!end

## Identification
- Cap 5–12 cm, smoky to olive-brown, with **pure white** warts; the edge has **fine grooves** (striations).
- Gills white, free. Spore print white.
- Stem white with a smooth, fragile ring (**no grooves** on its upper side).
- Bulbous base with a tight volva ending in a distinct **rolled rim ("gutter")**, often with one or two hoop-like rings just above.
- Flesh white and does **not** turn pink when cut or bruised (that is the blusher).

## Lookalikes
- **Deadly:** the [[s:death-cap]]: olive-green to yellowish cap, usually without warts, a skirt-like ring and a large **bag-like volva**.
- [[s:the-blusher]]: pinkish-brown cap with greyish or pinkish warts, **flesh turns pink** where damaged, **grooved ring**, no rolled rim at the base.
- [[s:fly-agaric]]: red cap.

## Dangers
- Contains **ibotenic acid and muscimol** in larger amounts than the fly agaric: severe poisoning, coma and sometimes death.
- **Impairment outdoors kills**: falls, wandering, drowning and hypothermia.
- Dangerous for **children and dogs**.

## Law
- Not a controlled drug under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971. The **Psychoactive Substances Act 2016** makes it an offence to produce, supply or offer to supply any psychoactive substance for people to consume.

## If someone has eaten it
**Symptoms:** usually start **30 minutes–2 hours** after eating and can last **12–24 hours**: dizziness, vomiting, confusion, alternating **deep sleepiness and agitation**, hallucinations, muscle jerks, fits and coma.

!danger Think "death cap" if
Symptoms start **6 hours or more** after eating, with **severe vomiting and watery diarrhoea**. They need hospital **urgently, even if they seem to recover**.
!end

**First aid:**
- **Don't** make them vomit. Sips of water only.
- **Keep a sample** (with the base) and a photo; note what, how much and when.
- **Stay with them**; keep them safe and **warm**.
- Drowsy or unresponsive: [[a:recovery-position]]; check breathing. Fits: [[a:seizures]]. Not breathing: CPR ([[t:cpr]]).
- This can be serious: **get them to medical help**, or send someone now. See [[a:poisoning]].

=== the-blusher
name: The blusher
latin: Amanita rubescens
group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: poisonous, caution
season: Jun–Nov
habitat: Broadleaf and conifer woods, very common
summary: Common Amanita with a pinkish-brown warty cap whose flesh slowly turns pink where damaged. Toxic raw; listed here because its lookalike is the dangerous panther cap.
lookalikes: panther-cap
tags: amanita, pink flesh, blusher
wiki: Amanita rubescens
---
## Identification
- Cap 5–15 cm, pinkish- to reddish-brown with greyish or pinkish (not pure white) warts.
- Gills white, stem with a ring that has fine grooves on its upper side.
- Flesh and maggot holes slowly **turn pink-red** where cut or bruised.

## Cautions
- It contains a toxin that is destroyed by thorough cooking, so some foragers eat it. It is too easily confused with the [[s:panther-cap]]. **Do not eat any Amanita.**

=== deadly-webcap
name: Deadly webcap
latin: Cortinarius rubellus
group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: deadly
season: Aug–Nov
habitat: Wet, acid conifer woods with moss (pine and spruce), especially in the Scottish Highlands
summary: Tawny-orange mushroom with a pointed cap, rusty gills and faint yellow bands on the stem. Destroys the kidneys days or weeks after eating.
lookalikes: chanterelle, fools-webcap
tags: webcap, cortinarius, orellanine, kidney failure, rusty spores
wiki: Cortinarius rubellus
---
!danger Deadly: delayed kidney failure
Contains orellanine. First symptoms appear **2–20 days** after eating, by which time the kidneys may be permanently damaged. People have mistaken it for chanterelles. Any suspected meal of webcaps needs a hospital: [[a:poisoning]].
!end

## Identification
- Cap 3–8 cm, tawny-orange to reddish-brown, dry, finely scaly, often with a **pointed central bump (umbo)**.
- Gills widely spaced, **rusty brown** (spore print rust-brown).
- Stem the same colour, with faint **yellowish bands** (remains of the web-like veil). Young caps have a cobweb-like veil (cortina) between the cap and the stem.

## Lookalikes
- [[s:chanterelle]] has no true gills (only blunt forked ridges) and an apricot smell.

group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: deadly
season: Mostly Sep–Dec, can appear in any month
habitat: In small groups or clusters on rotting wood, especially conifer stumps and logs

tags: galerina, amatoxin, little brown mushroom, wood, deadly

Contains amatoxins, as in the [[s:death-cap]]. A few caps can be fatal. Do not eat small brown mushrooms from wood.

- Thin brown stem with a small, thin, fragile ring that soon becomes a brownish band.

=== chanterelle
name: Chanterelle
latin: Cantharellus cibarius
aka: girolle
group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: edible
season: Jun–Nov
habitat: Mossy woodland, under beech, oak and birch, also in conifer woods; often on banks; common in Scotland and the west
summary: Egg-yolk yellow funnel with blunt, forked, vein-like ridges running down the stem instead of true gills, and a fruity apricot smell. Highly prized.
lookalikes: false-chanterelle, jack-o-lantern, deadly-webcap
tags: girolle, edible fungus, yellow, ridges, apricot smell
wiki: Cantharellus cibarius
---
!warn Expert confirmation needed
Check every specimen for **blunt, shallow, forked ridges**, not true thin gills. Check the rusty-gilled [[s:deadly-webcap]] has not crept into the basket. See [[a:compare-chanterelle]].
!end

## Identification
- Cap 3–10 cm, **egg-yolk yellow** to apricot, becoming wavy-edged and funnel-shaped; firm.
- Underside: **blunt, shallow, forked, vein-like ridges** running down the stem, the same colour as the cap. They do not peel away like gills.
- Stem solid, tapering down, same colour.
- Flesh **white to pale yellow inside**, firm, fibrous like string cheese when torn.
- Smell fruity, **like apricots**. Spore print pale cream.

## Uses
- Cook thoroughly; delicious. Cut, don't pull.

=== false-chanterelle
name: False chanterelle
latin: Hygrophoropsis aurantiaca
group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: poisonous, caution
season: Aug–Dec
habitat: Conifer woods, on needle litter, rotting wood, heathland
summary: Orange chanterelle lookalike with true, thin, crowded, repeatedly forked gills and soft flesh. Can cause stomach upset.
lookalikes: chanterelle
tags: lookalike, orange, gills
wiki: Hygrophoropsis aurantiaca
---
## Identification
- Cap 2–8 cm, **orange** (more orange than egg-yolk), often with a darker centre and inrolled edge, felty.
- **True gills**: thin, crowded, repeatedly forked, deeper orange than the cap.
- Thin, often curved stem; flesh soft and orange-tinged, without the apricot smell.

## Cautions
- Not seriously toxic, but can cause digestive upset and has been linked with hallucinations. Not worth eating. Its presence is the reason to check every chanterelle. See [[a:compare-chanterelle]].

=== jack-o-lantern
name: Jack o'lantern
latin: Omphalotus illudens
aka: Omphalotus olearius
group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: poisonous
season: Aug–Nov
habitat: Clustered on stumps or buried roots of broadleaf trees, especially oak and sweet chestnut; rare, southern England
summary: Bright orange mushroom growing in dense clusters from wood, with true gills. A chanterelle lookalike that causes severe vomiting.
lookalikes: chanterelle
tags: orange, clusters, wood, gills

---
## Identification
- Clusters of orange caps (5–15 cm) joined at the base, **on wood** (sometimes buried roots).
- True, sharp, crowded gills running down the stem; inner flesh orange (chanterelle flesh is white).

## Cautions
- Contains illudins: severe vomiting, cramps and diarrhoea. Chanterelles grow singly from the soil, never in tight clusters from stumps.

=== cep
name: Cep (penny bun)
latin: Boletus edulis
aka: porcini, king bolete
group: fungus
kind: bolete
status: edible
season: Aug–Nov
habitat: Broadleaf and conifer woods, especially with beech, oak, birch and pine
summary: Chunky bolete with a bread-roll brown cap, sponge-like pores (not gills) that start white, and a fat stem with a fine white net pattern. A top edible.
lookalikes: bitter-bolete, devils-bolete, bay-bolete
tags: porcini, bolete, pores, sponge, king bolete
wiki: Boletus edulis
---
## Identification
- Cap 8–25 cm, smooth, **bun-brown** (like a glazed bread roll), often with a paler edge.
- Underneath: **pores** (sponge) instead of gills, **white** when young, turning yellow then olive-green. They do **not** bruise blue.
- Stem fat and swollen, pale, with a fine **white raised net** (reticulum) at the top.
- Flesh white and unchanging when cut.

## Uses
- Excellent food. Slice to check for maggots; cook thoroughly; dries well.

## Bolete safety rule for beginners
- Avoid any bolete with **red or orange pores**, or flesh that turns **blue** when cut ([[s:devils-bolete]] and relatives). Taste a tiny piece raw and spit it out: if it is bitter it is the harmless but horribly bitter [[s:bitter-bolete]].

=== field-mushroom
name: Field mushroom
latin: Agaricus campestris
group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: edible, caution
season: Jul–Oct (after warm rain)
habitat: Grazed pasture and old grassland (horses, sheep), lawns; never in woods
summary: White cap, free gills that are bright pink when young turning chocolate brown, a thin ring and no volva. Never confuse it with white Amanitas.
lookalikes: yellow-stainer, death-cap, destroying-angel, horse-mushroom
tags: agaricus, mushroom, pink gills, grassland
wiki: Agaricus campestris
---
!warn Know the deadly white lookalikes
Young [[s:death-cap]] and [[s:destroying-angel]] are white with white gills, a volva and a ring. Field mushroom gills are **never white** once the cap opens: they are pink, then brown. Dig up the whole base; there must be no sac. Cut every mushroom: flesh that turns **chrome yellow** at the base means [[s:yellow-stainer]]. See [[a:compare-field-mushroom]].
!end

## Identification
- Cap 3–10 cm, white to cream, smooth or silky, sometimes with fine brown fibres.
- Gills free, crowded, **bright pink** in young caps, then **chocolate brown** to blackish. Spore print **dark brown**.
- Short stem with a thin, fragile, often disappearing ring; **no volva**.
- Flesh white, may blush slightly pinkish; pleasant mushroom smell.
- Grows in grassland, not woods.

## Uses
- Cook thoroughly. Discard old, maggoty or slimy specimens.

=== horse-mushroom

group: fungus
kind: mushroom
status: edible, caution
season: Jul–Nov
habitat: Pasture, meadows and grassy verges, sometimes in rings
summary: Large white Agaricus smelling of aniseed, with a cogwheel ring and gills that go from greyish-pink to dark brown. Easily confused with the yellow stainer.

- Cap 8–20 cm, white to creamy, yellowing slowly with age or handling.
- Gills free, greyish-pink becoming dark brown (never pure white in a mature cap).

- Smells of **aniseed/almond**.

=== giant-puffball
name: Giant puffball
latin: Calvatia gigantea
group: fungus
kind: puffball
status: edible
season: Jul–Oct
habitat: Fields, field margins, nettle beds, hedgerows, gardens and parkland
summary: A huge white ball (football-sized or bigger) with smooth leathery skin. Edible only while pure white and firm right through, like marshmallow.
lookalikes: common-earthball, death-cap
tags: puffball, white ball, edible fungus
wiki: Calvatia gigantea
---
## Identification
- Round white to cream ball, 10–80 cm across, with no stem, attached by a cord-like root.
- Inside: **uniformly pure white, firm and featureless**, like mozzarella or marshmallow.
- With age it turns yellow-olive inside, then a mass of brown spores, and the skin cracks.

## Uses
- Slice into steaks and fry. Eat only when pure white throughout.

## Cautions
- Always **cut in half vertically**. Small specimens must be checked for the outline of a cap and gills inside (an Amanita "egg"). A purple-black inside means [[s:common-earthball]].

=== common-puffball
name: Common puffball
latin: Lycoperdon perlatum
group: fungus
kind: puffball
status: edible, caution
season: Jul–Nov
habitat: Woodland floors, woodland edges and grassland, often in groups
summary: Small pear-shaped puffball covered in tiny pointed spines that rub off. Edible only when young, with white flesh and no gill outline inside.
lookalikes: common-earthball, death-cap
tags: puffball, pear shaped, spines
wiki: Lycoperdon perlatum
---
## Identification
- Pear- or club-shaped, 3–6 cm tall, white to cream, covered in **small conical spines** that rub off leaving a net pattern.
- A hole opens at the top of old specimens, puffing out spores.
- Inside: white and firm when young; olive and powdery later.

## Cautions
- **Slice each one top to bottom.** It must be uniform white with no outline of a cap or gills (a young deadly Amanita), and not purple-black ([[s:common-earthball]]).
