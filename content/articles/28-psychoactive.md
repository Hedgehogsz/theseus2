!quick
1. **Never eat** a wild mushroom or plant for its effects. Deadly lookalikes grow alongside them, and being impaired outdoors kills.
2. If someone has eaten one: **don't make them vomit**. **Keep a sample** and a photo; note **what, how much and when**.
3. Keep them **safe, warm and watched**: away from water, edges and fire. Drowsy: [[a:recovery-position]]. Not breathing: CPR.
4. **Symptoms starting 6+ hours after a mushroom** = possible death cap-type poison: get them to **hospital urgently**, even if they seem to recover.
!end

## Why this guide covers them
This section is here so you can **recognise** these species, **avoid** them, protect children and dogs, know the law, and give **first aid** if someone has eaten one. It contains no information on using them, and never will.

The two big killers are:
- **Misidentification.** Magic mushrooms are small brown mushrooms that grow in the same fields, lawns and woodchip as [[s:pholiotina-rugosa]] and Galerina species such as the [[s:funeral-bell]]. These contain **amatoxins**, the poison in the [[s:death-cap]]. There are no symptoms for 6–24 hours, then the liver fails. The [[s:fly-agaric]] and [[s:panther-cap]] are in the same family as the death cap and destroying angel. See [[a:compare-liberty-cap]] and [[a:compare-amanitas]].
- **Accidental poisoning.** Children eat berries, seeds, mushrooms from lawns and cannabis edibles; adults mistake [[s:henbane]] or [[s:mandrake]] leaves for greens, or eat grain containing [[s:ergot]].

## Impairment kills outdoors
In a survival situation your judgement is your most important piece of kit. Anything that alters the mind takes it away for hours or days:
- **Falls** from crags, banks and steep ground; walking off in the dark.
- **Hypothermia**: confused or drowsy people don't notice the cold, take off clothing, or lie still on cold ground. See [[a:hypothermia]].
- **Drowning**: walking into rivers, lakes or the sea; the cold shock does the rest.
- **Getting lost**: wandering away from shelter or the group.
- **Fire and burns**: falling into or neglecting a fire.
- **Bad decisions**: abandoning a plan, panicking, failing to signal or ration.
- Someone else must stop everything to look after them, often for a day or more.

## UK law in brief
!law The law across the UK
- **Misuse of Drugs Act 1971.** Mushrooms containing psilocin or psilocybin ([[s:liberty-cap]], [[s:wavy-cap]], [[s:banded-mottlegill]]) are **Class A**, **fresh or dried**: the Drugs Act 2005 made this explicit. **Opium** and **poppy straw** from the [[s:opium-poppy]] are Class A. [[s:cannabis]] is **Class B**, and growing it without a Home Office licence is an offence.
- **Penalties** (maximum): Class A possession up to 7 years in prison, supply or production up to life; Class B possession up to 5 years, supply or production up to 14 years.
- **Psychoactive Substances Act 2016.** It is an offence to produce, supply, offer to supply, import or export any psychoactive substance for people to consume (up to 7 years). This covers things not listed as controlled drugs, including the [[s:fly-agaric]], [[s:panther-cap]] and plants such as [[s:thorn-apple]]. Food (such as [[s:nutmeg]]), alcohol, caffeine, nicotine and medicines are exempt.
- **Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981.** Uprooting any wild plant without the landowner's permission is illegal.
!end

## Recognising poisoning
Note when symptoms started: the timing is one of the best clues.

| Type | Main species | Starts | Signs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anticholinergic** | [[s:thorn-apple]], [[s:henbane]], [[s:deadly-nightshade]], [[s:angels-trumpet]], [[s:mandrake]] | 30 min–2 h | **Hot, dry, flushed** skin; **very large pupils**; fast heart; can't pass urine; delirium, picking at the air. "Hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter." Lasts 1–3 days |
| **Psilocybin-type** | [[s:liberty-cap]], [[s:wavy-cap]], [[s:banded-mottlegill]] | 20–60 min | Large pupils, nausea, confusion, hallucinations, panic, poor coordination. Lasts 4–8 h |
| **Muscimol (Amanita)** | [[s:fly-agaric]], [[s:panther-cap]] | 30 min–2 h | Vomiting, dizziness, **deep sleepiness alternating with agitation**, muscle jerks, fits, coma |
| **Opioid** | [[s:opium-poppy]] | 30 min–2 h | **Pinpoint pupils**, very drowsy, **slow or shallow breathing**, blue lips |
| **Muscarine** | [[s:deadly-fibrecap]] and other fibrecaps | 15 min–2 h | **Sweating, drooling, tears**, slow pulse, small pupils, breathing difficulty |
| **Amatoxin** | [[s:death-cap]], [[s:funeral-bell]], [[s:pholiotina-rugosa]] | **6–24 h** | Violent vomiting and watery diarrhoea, then a false recovery while the liver fails |
| **Nutmeg** | [[s:nutmeg]] | 3–8 h | Nausea, palpitations, dry mouth, anxiety, confusion. Lasts 1–2 days |

!danger Mixed or delayed symptoms
Someone who ate a "magic mushroom" and then develops vomiting and watery diarrhoea **6 hours or more** later may have eaten a deadly lookalike. They need hospital **urgently, even if they recover**: see [[a:poisoning]].
!end

## First aid
1. **Make the area safe.** Move them away from water, edges, roads and fire. Take any remaining plant or mushroom away from them.
2. **Check response and breathing** ([[a:primary-survey]]). Not breathing normally: CPR ([[t:cpr]]). Breathing but drowsy: [[a:recovery-position]], and keep checking breathing.
3. **Don't make them vomit**, and give nothing but sips of water. No alcohol or other drugs.
4. **Keep a sample** (the whole mushroom with its base, a sprig with leaves, flowers and berries, or vomit) in paper or cloth, and photograph it. Write down **what, how much and when** in [[t:casualty]].
5. **Keep them warm**: insulate them from the ground, add layers, use a survival bag. If they are **overheating** (anticholinergic poisoning), loosen clothing and cool gently with damp cloths, then guard against chilling.
6. **Stay with them.** Speak calmly and simply, one person at a time; keep light and noise low. Don't restrain them unless they are about to hurt themselves.
7. Fits: protect the head, don't hold them down ([[a:seizures]]).
8. **Get them to medical help.** Send someone, or move them once it is safe. When you reach a phone or a person, ask for an ambulance and say what was eaten and when ([[a:when-you-reach-help]]). For anyone injured too, follow [[f:injured]].

## Children and pets
- Teach children **never to eat anything** from the garden, park or countryside without asking. Clear fungi from lawns where small children play.
- Even a small amount can seriously poison a child: [[s:deadly-nightshade]] berries, [[s:thorn-apple]] seeds, [[s:angels-trumpet]] flowers, mushrooms from lawns and woodchip.
- Keep **cannabis edibles**, medicines and spices such as nutmeg out of reach.
- **Dogs** often eat mushrooms (including fly agaric and panther cap), cannabis edibles and garden plants. Keep a sample and get them to a vet.

## Species in this section

- Often confused with these but **not** psychoactive: [[s:hemlock-water-dropwort]], which is deadly.

!quick
1. **Never eat a little brown mushroom.** Several deadly species look almost the same and grow in the same grass, moss and woodchip.
2. If someone has eaten one, **note the time**. Symptoms starting **6 hours or more** later = possible **amatoxins**: get them to **hospital urgently**, even if they recover.
3. **Sweating, drooling and tears** within 2 hours = possible **muscarine**: get them to medical help now.
4. **Keep a sample** of what they ate and any left in the patch, in paper. Hospitals use it to identify the species.
!end

## Why it's a deadly gamble
"Little brown mushrooms" (LBMs) are hundreds of small, brown, similar-looking species. Many have never been tested for toxins. Experts often need a **microscope** to tell them apart. Several of them kill:
- **Amatoxins** (the same poison as the [[s:death-cap]]): the [[s:funeral-bell]] and other Galerina species, and the deadly conecap [[s:pholiotina-rugosa]]. Cooking, drying and freezing don't destroy them.
- **Muscarine**: many brown **fibrecaps** (Inocybe), such as the [[s:deadly-fibrecap]].
- People looking for the [[s:liberty-cap]] pick a handful of small brown caps in poor light. It only takes **one or two** of the wrong ones mixed in.

## Side by side
| Feature | [[s:liberty-cap]] | [[s:funeral-bell]] | [[s:pholiotina-rugosa]] | Fibrecaps ([[s:deadly-fibrecap]]) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cap | 0.5–2.5 cm, **conical with a sharp nipple**, never opens flat | 1–5 cm, convex then flat | 0.5–2.5 cm, conical to bell-shaped | 2–8 cm, **conical, radially fibrous**, edge often **splits** |
| Colour | Chestnut to olive-brown wet, **cream when dry** | Honey to tawny brown, drying paler | Orange-brown to rusty tawny | Brown, straw or cream; deadly fibrecap **bruises brick-red** |
| Gills | Grey-brown to **purple-black** | Yellowish to **rusty brown** | Pale ochre to **rusty brown** | Pale to clay or olive-brown |
| Ring | **None** | Thin ring or brown band | **Membranous ring**, may fall off | None |
| Spore print | **Dark purple-brown** | **Rusty brown** | **Rusty brown** | Dull brown |
| Grows | In **grass**: pasture, lawns | On **wood**; other Galerinas **in moss and grass** | **Woodchip, lawns**, gardens | Soil **under trees**, parks, path edges |
| Season | Sep–Nov | Sep–Dec | Jun–Nov | May–Nov |
| Poison | Psilocybin (**Class A**) | **Amatoxins: deadly** | **Amatoxins: deadly** | **Muscarine: can kill** |
| Symptoms start | 20–60 min | **6–24 h** | **6–24 h** | 15 min–2 h |

!warn No single feature is safe
Blue bruising, a nipple on the cap, or a dark spore print do **not** make a mushroom safe. Features overlap, caps change colour as they dry, rings fall off, and deadly species grow **in the same patch**.
!end

## Spore print colours
A spore print helps identify what someone **has eaten** (see [[a:fungi-id-basics]] for how). Lay a cap gills-down on paper, half white and half dark, for 2–12 hours.
- **Rusty brown or orange-brown**: Galerina, Pholiotina, webcaps ([[s:deadly-webcap]]), rustgills ([[s:spectacular-rustgill]]). Several are **deadly**.
- **Dull brown**: fibrecaps (Inocybe).
- **Purple-brown to purple-black**: Psilocybe, also [[s:sulphur-tuft]] and field mushrooms.
- **Black**: mottlegills ([[s:banded-mottlegill]]) and inkcaps.
- **White**: Amanitas ([[s:death-cap]], [[s:panther-cap]]) and many others.

## Delayed symptoms
Amatoxin poisoning follows a pattern:
1. **6–24 hours**: nothing. They feel fine.
2. **Then**: violent vomiting, stomach cramps and profuse watery diarrhoea, causing dehydration.
3. **After about a day**: a **false recovery**.
4. **2–5 days**: liver and kidney failure.

Hospital treatment works best **early**. Don't wait for symptoms: anyone who may have eaten a little brown mushroom needs medical help. Give small, frequent sips of fluid if they are vomiting ([[a:diarrhoea-ors]]). See [[a:poisoning]].

!quick
1. **Never eat any Amanita.** The family includes the [[s:death-cap]] and [[s:destroying-angel]], which cause most mushroom deaths.
2. Amanita signs: **white gills**, a **ring** on the stem, **warts** or patches on the cap, and a **bulb or volva** at the base. **Dig up the whole base.**
3. Panther cap vs blusher: **blusher flesh turns pink** where cut or nibbled; panther cap stays **white**.
4. Eaten one? Symptoms **6+ hours later** with vomiting and watery diarrhoea = possible death cap: **hospital urgently**.
!end

## The Amanita family
Amanitas start as a white **"egg"** wrapped in a skin (the universal veil). As the mushroom grows it tears this skin, leaving:
- **Warts or patches** on the cap (rain can wash them off).
- A **volva** at the base: a loose bag in the [[s:death-cap]] and [[s:destroying-angel]], a rimmed cup in the [[s:panther-cap]], rings of warty collars in the [[s:fly-agaric]]. It is often **buried**: twist and lift the whole mushroom, don't cut it.

Most also have a **ring** (skirt) on the stem and **white gills** and spore print. If you see these together, you are looking at an Amanita.

## Side by side
| Feature | [[s:fly-agaric]] | [[s:panther-cap]] | [[s:the-blusher]] | [[s:death-cap]] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cap | **Scarlet to orange**, white warts | **Smoky to olive-brown**, **pure white** warts | **Pinkish to reddish-brown**, greyish or pinkish warts | **Olive-green to yellowish**, sometimes pale; usually **no warts** |
| Cap edge | Finely grooved with age | **Finely grooved** | Smooth | Smooth |
| Ring | White, hanging | Smooth, fragile, **no grooves** | **Grooved** on its upper side | Drooping skirt |
| Base | Bulb with **warty collars** | Bulb with a tight volva with a **rolled rim ("gutter")** | Swollen bulb, no distinct rim, often pinkish | Large **bag-like volva** |
| Cut or bruised flesh | White | **Stays white** | **Turns pink-red** slowly | White |
| Where | Birch and pine | Beech and oak, often chalk | Almost any woodland | Oak and beech |
| Season | Aug–Nov | Jul–Oct | Jun–Nov | Jul–Nov |
| Poison | Ibotenic acid, muscimol | **Same, stronger: has killed** | Toxic raw | **Amatoxins: deadly** |
| Symptoms start | 30 min–2 h | 30 min–2 h | Within hours | **6–24 h** |

## Panther cap vs blusher
These two are the most often confused, because [[s:the-blusher]] is sometimes eaten after thorough cooking. **Don't.**
- **Flesh**: cut the stem and cap, and look at insect holes. The blusher slowly turns **pink to wine-red**; the panther cap stays **white**.
- **Warts**: panther cap **pure white**; blusher greyish, dirty-white or pinkish.
- **Ring**: blusher ring has **fine grooves** on top; panther cap ring is **smooth**.
- **Cap edge**: panther cap has a **finely grooved** edge; blusher is smooth.
- **Base**: panther cap has a neat **rolled rim** to the volva; the blusher has a rough, swollen base without one.
- Old, rain-washed or nibbled specimens blur every one of these features.

## Always think "death cap"
- A fly agaric or panther cap with its warts washed off, or a pale specimen, can look like other Amanitas.
- **Young "eggs"** of all Amanitas look alike, and like puffballs: cut them in half; an outline of a cap and gills means Amanita.
- The [[s:destroying-angel]] is **pure white**, with a ring and bag-like volva.
- See [[a:compare-field-mushroom]] for the white Amanitas.

## If someone has eaten an Amanita
- **Fly agaric or panther cap**: within 30 minutes–2 hours, dizziness, vomiting, confusion, deep sleepiness alternating with agitation, muscle jerks, fits. Keep them safe, warm and watched; [[a:recovery-position]] if drowsy; [[a:seizures]] for fits.
- **Any symptoms starting 6 hours or more later**, with vomiting and watery diarrhoea: treat as **death cap** poisoning. Hospital **urgently**, even if they recover.
- **Don't** make them vomit. **Keep a sample with its base**, and a photo. Get them to medical help. See [[a:poisoning]] and [[a:psychoactive-safety]].

## Law
- Fly agaric and panther cap are **not** controlled under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971, but the **Psychoactive Substances Act 2016** makes it an offence to produce or supply them, or any psychoactive substance, for people to consume.
