# Quick reference: distress signals, Morse code, the phonetic alphabet and conversions.

=== distress-signals
title: Distress signals at a glance
summary: Every common distress signal on one page: whistle and light, SOS, ground-to-air letters, body signals to aircraft, and signals at sea.
tags: distress signal, SOS, six blasts, whistle, torch, ground to air, V, X, body signals, helicopter, aircraft, flare, orange smoke, mayday, international mountain distress signal
icon: triangle-alert
related: signalling-rescue, helicopter-rescue, whistle-and-sound, be-visible
---
!quick
1. **6** whistle blasts or torch flashes spread over a minute, a minute's pause, repeat. The **reply is 3**.
2. **SOS:** · · · – – – · · · (3 short, 3 long, 3 short) in sound or light. The [[t:signal]] tool does it for you.
3. On the ground: **V** = need assistance, **X** = need medical help. Each line 3 m or longer.
4. To a helicopter: **both arms up in a Y** = yes, we need help.
!end

## Sound and light
| Signal | Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| International Mountain Distress Signal | **6** long blasts or flashes, evenly spread over 1 minute; 1 minute's pause; repeat | I need help |
| Reply | **3** blasts or flashes in a minute; pause; repeat | We've heard you |
| SOS | · · · – – – · · · (3 short, 3 long, 3 short), sent as one run; pause; repeat | Distress, understood worldwide |

- Keep signalling until rescuers reach you, so they can home in on you.
- By torch at night, or mirror flash in sun. The phone's torch, screen and sound beacons are in [[t:signal]]; Morse is in [[t:morse]] and [[a:morse-code]].

## Ground-to-air signals
!svg ground-to-air

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **V** | We need assistance |
| **X** | We need medical help |
| **N** | No |
| **Y** | Yes |
| **Arrow** | We have gone this way |
| **SOS** | Distress |

- Make each line **at least 3 m long** (bigger is better), straight-edged, on open ground.
- Use **contrast**: dark rocks, logs or turned turf on grass or snow; bright kit or a tarp on dark ground.

## Body signals to aircraft
| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Both arms raised above your head in a **Y** | **Yes**: we need help, land here |
| One arm raised, the other down by your side | **No**: we don't need help |

- **Don't wave** casually at a helicopter: a one-armed wave can look like "no help needed".
- When it lands: crouch, stay still, protect your eyes from downdraught, and wait for the crew to come to you ([[a:helicopter-rescue]]).

## At sea and on the coast
| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Red flare (hand-held or parachute rocket) | Distress |
| Orange smoke | Distress, by day |
| Arms outstretched to the sides, slowly and repeatedly raised and lowered | Distress |
| SOS by light or sound | Distress |
| Continuous sounding of a horn or foghorn | Distress |
| "Mayday" spoken three times on VHF radio, channel 16 | Distress: grave and immediate danger to life |
| "Pan-pan" on VHF radio | Urgent, but not immediately life-threatening |

- Hold a hand flare at arm's length, downwind and pointing away from you. Never fire a rocket flare when a helicopter is close.

=== morse-code
title: Morse code
summary: The full Morse alphabet and numbers, the prosigns worth knowing, and the timing rules for sending by torch, whistle or tapping.
tags: morse, morse code, dots and dashes, dits and dahs, SOS, signalling, torch, tapping, prosigns, alphabet
icon: radio
related: distress-signals, signalling-rescue, nato-alphabet
---
!quick
1. **SOS** = · · · – – – · · · sent as one run, with no letter gaps.
2. **Timing:** dot = 1 unit; dash = **3**; gap inside a letter = 1; gap between letters = **3**; between words = **7**.
3. By torch or whistle, make one unit about **half a second**. Slow and clear beats fast.
4. The [[t:morse]] tool can send it for you.
!end

## Prosigns and punctuation
| Sign | Code | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| SOS | · · · – – – · · · | Distress (sent as one character) |
| AR | · – · – · | End of message |
| SK | · · · – · – | End of contact: signing off |
| K | – · – | Over: your turn to send |
| R | · – · | Received |
| AS | · – · · · | Wait |
| BT | – · · · – | Break: new section |
| Error | · · · · · · · · | Mistake: 8 dots, then resend the last word |
| Full stop | · – · – · – | . |
| Question mark | · · – – · · | ? |

## Timing
| Element | Length |
|---|---|
| Dot | 1 unit |
| Dash | 3 units |
| Gap between dots and dashes in a letter | 1 unit |
| Gap between letters | 3 units |
| Gap between words | 7 units |

- With a torch, half a second per unit is about 2–3 words a minute: slow, but readable at a distance. Speed up only if the other person can follow.
- By sound: short and long whistle blasts, or taps on a pipe or a wall (a tap for a dot, a scrape or a double tap for a dash, if you agree that first).
- **Receiving:** write the dots and dashes down as they come, with a slash between letters, then decode. Ask for a repeat by sending the question mark.
- To learn: start with SOS, then the short common letters (E, T, A, I, N, O, S), then the letters of your name.

!quick
1. **Letters:** Alfa, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee, Zulu.
2. **Numbers** one digit at a time: say **zero**, never "oh"; nine is **niner**.
3. **Grid reference:** the two letters phonetically, then each digit, then repeat it all: "November Yankee, two one five, zero seven two".
!end

Say each digit separately ("one five zero", not "a hundred and fifty"), except round hundreds and thousands. Give times in the 24-hour clock: "one four three zero".

## Spelling a grid reference
1. Read it off the map or [[t:gps]] and **write it down** first ([[a:grid-references]]).
2. Say "**Grid reference**", then the two letters phonetically, then the digits in two groups: "November Yankee, two one five, zero seven two".
3. **Repeat it**, and ask the listener to **read it back** to you. Correct any mistake with "Correction", then the right figures.
4. Add a description: "on the north bank of a stream, 200 metres east of a sheepfold". See [[a:location-report]].

To spell a word, say "I spell", then the letters: "Smith, I spell Sierra Mike India Tango Hotel".

!quick
1. **Distance:** 1 mile = **1.6 km**; 1 km = **0.62 mile** (8 km ≈ 5 miles). 1 m ≈ **3.3 ft**.
2. **Temperature:** °F ≈ **°C × 2 + 30** (exactly × 1.8 + 32). 0 °C = 32 °F; 37 °C = 98.6 °F.
3. **Volume:** 1 litre ≈ **1¾ UK pints**; 1 UK gallon = **4.5 litres** (a US gallon is 3.8). A litre of water weighs 1 kg.
4. **Wind:** a Force 8 gale is **39–46 mph** (34–40 knots). [[t:calc]] converts any of these for you.
!end

## Beaufort wind scale
| Force | Name | mph | knots | km/h | On land |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Calm | under 1 | under 1 | under 1 | Smoke rises straight up |
| 1 | Light air | 1–3 | 1–3 | 1–5 | Smoke drifts |
| 2 | Light breeze | 4–7 | 4–6 | 6–11 | Leaves rustle; wind felt on the face |
| 3 | Gentle breeze | 8–12 | 7–10 | 12–19 | Leaves and twigs move constantly |
| 4 | Moderate breeze | 13–18 | 11–16 | 20–28 | Dust and loose paper lift; small branches move |
| 5 | Fresh breeze | 19–24 | 17–21 | 29–38 | Small trees in leaf sway |
| 6 | Strong breeze | 25–31 | 22–27 | 39–49 | Large branches move; umbrellas hard to use |
| 7 | Near gale | 32–38 | 28–33 | 50–61 | Whole trees move; hard to walk into the wind |
| 8 | Gale | 39–46 | 34–40 | 62–74 | Twigs break off; walking is impeded |
| 9 | Strong gale | 47–54 | 41–47 | 75–88 | Slates and chimney pots blown off |
| 10 | Storm | 55–63 | 48–55 | 89–102 | Trees uprooted; serious damage |
| 11 | Violent storm | 64–72 | 56–63 | 103–117 | Widespread damage |
| 12 | Hurricane force | 73+ | 64+ | 118+ | Devastation |

Summit winds are often much stronger than in the valley. Mountain forecasts give summit wind speeds: roughly, 30 mph makes walking tiring, 40 mph makes it difficult, and 50 mph or more can blow you over. Wind chill: [[a:wind-chill]].

## Boiling water (rough guide)
| Situation | Time to boil 1 litre |
|---|---|
| Gas stove, still air, lid on | about 4–5 min |
| Good fire of dry hardwood, pot on embers, lid on | about 5–10 min |
| Damp wood, wind, no lid | 15 min or more |

- A lid and a windbreak save a lot of time and fuel.
- Water boils about 1 °C cooler for every 300 m of height: about 95.5 °C on top of Ben Nevis (1,345 m). That's still hot enough: a **rolling boil** makes water safe anywhere in Britain ([[a:boiling-limits]], [[t:boil]]).
