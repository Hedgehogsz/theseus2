# Food & foraging. Food is the lowest survival priority in the UK; identification mistakes kill.

=== foraging-rules
title: Universal foraging rules (read first)
summary: The non-negotiable rules for eating anything wild in the UK. This app is not a substitute for expert identification.
tags: foraging, rules, identification, safety, wild food, disclaimer, fungi, berries
---
!quick
1. **If in doubt, leave it out.** Certainty, not "probably".
2. **Never eat any carrot-family plant** (white umbrella flowers, lacy leaves) unless you are an expert: [[a:compare-cow-parsley]].
3. **Never eat wild mushrooms** on your own judgement: [[a:fungi-id-basics]].
4. Start with **one** new food, a **small amount**, cooked. Keep a sample in case you are ill.
!end

!danger This app is not a substitute for expert identification
Never eat any wild plant, berry or fungus unless you are **100% certain** what it is. Use **several** features (leaf, stem, flower, smell, habitat, season), never one. Never eat **wild fungi** unless an expert has confirmed the identification in person. Several UK species kill, and some cause no symptoms until the damage is done.
!end

## The rules
1. **Identify positively**, using every feature in the species entry, and check the lookalikes listed on each page.
2. **Know the deadly ones first.** Learn [[s:hemlock]], [[s:hemlock-water-dropwort]], [[s:death-cap]], [[s:destroying-angel]], [[s:yew]], [[s:deadly-nightshade]], [[s:foxglove]], [[s:lords-and-ladies]] and [[s:lily-of-the-valley]] before anything else.
3. **Pick carefully:** leaf by leaf among mixed plants (wild garlic beds contain poisonous leaves).
4. **Location:** avoid roadsides (exhaust, salt), field edges (sprays), dog-walking paths, polluted water and contaminated land.
5. **Cook** where the entry says so (nettles, elderberries, rowan, watercress, all fungi).
6. **Start small:** a little, then wait 24 hours. Some people react even to safe foods.
7. **Never rely on tests or myths:** "animals eat it", "it doesn't taste bitter", "silver spoon turns black" and the so-called universal edibility test are not safe. See [[a:edibility-test-myth]].
8. **The law:** you may pick leaves, flowers, fruit and fungi for personal use from land where you have legal access, but **uprooting** any wild plant without the landowner's permission is illegal. Some species are protected (e.g. [[s:bluebell]]). Byelaws apply in some places.

## Remember the priorities
In Britain you will be rescued, or walk out, long before starvation matters. A wrong mushroom can kill you; hunger won't, for weeks. See [[a:food-priorities]].

=== food-priorities
title: Food: how much does it matter?
summary: Why food is the last priority in a UK survival situation, and how to make what you carry last.
tags: food, hunger, calories, starvation, priorities, energy
---
!quick
1. A healthy adult can survive about **3 weeks** without food (with water). It is the **last** priority.
2. Hunger lowers morale and warmth: **eat what you carry**, in small regular amounts, especially before sleeping.
3. Spend energy on **shelter, water and being found**, not on chasing food.
4. Wild food in the UK is low in calories; it rarely repays the energy spent gathering it, except nuts, berries and fish.
!end

## Notes
- If water is scarce, eat less (digestion uses water, especially protein).
- Most calorie-rich UK wild foods: [[s:hazel]] nuts, [[s:sweet-chestnut]], beechnuts ([[s:beech]], small amounts), leached acorns ([[s:oak]]), blackberries, bilberries, fish and shellfish (with care).
- Greens (nettle, dandelion, wild garlic) add vitamins and morale, but very few calories.

=== edibility-test-myth
title: Why the "universal edibility test" is dangerous
summary: The skin-lip-tongue test from military manuals does not protect you from the UK's deadliest plants and fungi.
tags: universal edibility test, myth, taste test, poison, survival manual
---
!quick
1. **Don't use the "universal edibility test" in Britain.** Many deadly plants and all deadly fungi taste fine and cause no early symptoms.
2. Eat only what you can **positively identify**.
3. If you have no safe food, **go without**: you have weeks.
!end

## Why it fails
- [[s:death-cap]] reportedly tastes pleasant, and its symptoms are delayed 6–24 hours. By then the liver damage is under way.
- [[s:hemlock-water-dropwort]] roots taste sweet like parsnip; a small piece can kill within hours.
- [[s:yew]] seeds and leaves can stop the heart with few warning signs.
- Some poisons are cumulative or delayed for days ([[s:deadly-webcap]]).

=== trapping-law
title: Trapping, snaring and hunting: the UK law
summary: Most trapping and snaring in the UK is illegal or tightly regulated. What the law allows, and why it is the wrong priority anyway.
tags: trapping, snares, traps, hunting, law, poaching, wildlife and countryside act, legal
---
!quick
1. Don't set traps or snares. In Britain it is almost certainly **illegal** for you, and rescue will come long before you need the meat.
2. Food is the **last** survival priority in the UK ([[a:food-priorities]]).
3. Legal, low-energy food: nuts, berries, greens, and fishing **with a licence and permission**.
!end

!law The law in brief
- **All wild birds** and many mammals are protected (Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 and similar laws). Killing or taking them is an offence except for listed species, by authorised people, under licence.
- **Snares** are **banned in Wales** (Agriculture (Wales) Act 2023) and **Scotland** (Wildlife Management and Muirburn (Scotland) Act 2024). In England and Northern Ireland, only free-running snares are legal, set by authorised people, with strict conditions. **Self-locking snares are illegal everywhere.**
- **Spring traps** must be of an approved type, used only for listed species and set to the rules. **Glue traps** are banned for public use.
- Taking **game** (pheasant, partridge, deer, hares) without the shooting rights is **poaching**, a criminal offence. Deer are protected by close seasons and firearm rules.
- **Fishing** needs a licence and permission: see [[a:fishing-basics]].
!end

## Why this matters
- UK trapping law exists for animal welfare: badly set traps and snares catch pets, protected species and livestock, and cause suffering.
- Even landowners and gamekeepers must follow codes of practice, use approved traps and check them at least once a day.
- In a genuine life-threatening emergency the law still applies. The courts decide whether a defence of necessity holds, which is rare and uncertain. The practical answer is that it is never necessary in Britain.

## Sources
- Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 (legislation.gov.uk)
- Agriculture (Wales) Act 2023, section on snares and glue traps
- Wildlife Management and Muirburn (Scotland) Act 2024
- Glue Traps (Offences) Act 2022
- DEFRA: Code of practice on the use of snares (England); Spring Traps Approval Order
- Deer Act 1991; Game Act 1831; Night Poaching Act 1828

=== fishing-basics
title: Fishing basics and the law
summary: Simple hook-and-line fishing, improvised kit, preparing fish, and the UK licence and permission rules.
tags: fishing, rod licence, hook, line, trout, mackerel, sea fishing, bait, law
---
!quick
1. **Freshwater (England and Wales):** you need an **Environment Agency rod licence** *and* the fishing rights owner's permission. Scotland: no licence, but you still need permission. Observe close seasons.
2. **Sea fishing** from the shore needs no licence in the UK (but minimum sizes, bag limits and byelaws apply).
3. Simple kit: line, small hook, weight, bait (worms, insects, bread). Fish dawn and dusk, near cover.
4. **Gut and cook thoroughly**; eat the same day.
!end

## Where fish are
- Rivers: below riffles, at the tail of pools, under undercut banks, behind boulders, and in the shade of trees.
- Sea: from rocks, piers and beaches on a rising tide; [[s:mackerel]] shoals in summer.

## Improvised tackle
- Hooks: a bent safety pin, a thorn ([[s:hawthorn]], [[s:blackthorn]]), or a carved bone or wood "gorge" (a short stick sharpened at both ends, tied in the middle, swallowed lengthways).
- Line: strong thread or fine cordage; a stick as a rod.
- Nets, traps, night lines, explosives and poisons are illegal for freshwater fish without specific authorisation.

## Preparing fish
- Kill quickly with a sharp blow to the top of the head.
- Gut: slit the belly from vent to gills, remove the insides, rinse in clean water, and remove the gills.
- Cook thoroughly over embers or in the pot. Freshwater fish carry parasites: no raw fish.

=== insects
title: Insects and invertebrates as food
summary: Which UK invertebrates are safe to eat cooked, which to avoid, and why they aren't worth much effort.
tags: insects, grubs, worms, woodlice, grasshoppers, protein, bugs
---
!quick
1. **Always cook** (boil or roast): parasites and bacteria.
2. Reasonable: **earthworms** (purge in clean water, then boil), **grasshoppers and crickets** (remove legs and wings), **beetle larvae** from rotten wood, **woodlice** (boil; related to shrimps).
3. **Avoid:** hairy caterpillars, brightly coloured insects, anything that stinks or bites, slugs and snails (parasites), carrion flies, ticks and spiders.
!end

## Notes
- High protein, but only in tiny amounts. Turning over logs costs energy; look under logs and stones you are moving anyway.
- Shellfish allergy sufferers may react to insects and woodlice.
- Snails can carry parasites such as rat lungworm. Only purged, thoroughly cooked edible snails are eaten in Europe; best avoided.

- Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO): Edible insects
- Food Standards Agency: Edible insects guidance

=== cooking-wild-food
title: Preparing wild food safely
summary: Washing, cooking and storing wild food with limited kit to avoid stomach upsets.
tags: food hygiene, cooking, wild food, storage, washing
---
!quick
1. **Wash** greens in boiled or clean water; discard yellow, slimy or insect-damaged leaves.
2. **Boil or cook** anything doubtful; boiling is the safest single method.
3. Eat foraged food **fresh**; don't store cooked food without refrigeration for more than a day.
4. Keep a **sample** of each new wild food in case someone becomes ill.
!end

## Notes
- Wild garlic, nettles and other greens wilt in seconds in boiling water: short cooking keeps the vitamins.
- Rinse berries; pick above dog height.
- Fat and salt make food keep better; dried food (slices of fruit, mushrooms) stores well if fully dry.

## Sources
- Food Standards Agency: Food hygiene (food.gov.uk)
- Woodland Trust: Foraging guidelines

=== shellfish-shore
title: Seashore food and shellfish safety
summary: The coast is the UK's richest wild larder, but shellfish poisoning and tides are serious risks.
tags: shellfish, mussels, winkles, seaweed, coast, foraging, red tide
---
!quick
1. Only gather from **clean, open coasts**, away from harbours, outfalls and river mouths; not after heavy rain.
2. **Shellfish poisoning toxins are not destroyed by cooking.** Avoid shellfish during algal blooms (discoloured water).
3. Cook everything: [[s:mussel]] until the shells open (discard any that stay shut); [[s:periwinkle]] for 3–5 minutes.
4. Watch the tide ([[a:coast-and-tides]]).
!end

## Seaweeds
- Most UK seaweeds are edible when fresh and attached to rock (never washed-up rotting weed). Common choices: dulse, sea lettuce, bladderwrack (in small amounts). Rinse well and cook if unsure.
- Avoid seaweed from polluted water.
