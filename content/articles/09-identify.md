# Identification guides and deadly lookalike comparisons. Compare pages show the species side by side with photos.

=== compare-wild-garlic
title: Wild garlic vs lily of the valley vs lords-and-ladies
summary: Side-by-side comparison of wild garlic and the poisonous leaves that grow among it.
tags: wild garlic, ramsons, lily of the valley, lords and ladies, arum, lookalike, comparison, leaves
compare: wild-garlic, lily-of-the-valley, lords-and-ladies, bluebell
icon: leafy-green
---
!quick
1. **Smell every leaf** (crush it): wild garlic smells strongly of garlic. No smell = don't eat. Once your hands smell of garlic, check leaf shape instead.
2. Wild garlic: **one flat, soft leaf per stalk**, coming straight from the ground, with **parallel veins**.
3. **Arrow-shaped, net-veined, glossy or spotted** = lords-and-ladies. **Two leaves wrapped round one stem** = lily of the valley.
4. Pick leaf by leaf, never in handfuls.
!end

## Side by side
| Feature | [[s:wild-garlic]] | [[s:lily-of-the-valley]] | [[s:lords-and-ladies]] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smell when crushed | **Strong garlic** | None | None (unpleasant) |
| Leaf shape | Elliptical, pointed, soft, flat | Broad elliptical, firmer | **Arrow-shaped** with backward lobes |
| Veins | Parallel | Parallel | **Branching net** |
| How leaves grow | **Each leaf on its own stalk** from the ground | **2–3 leaves wrapped round one stem** | Leaf stalks wrapped at the base; leaves often **dark-spotted** |
| Flowers | White stars in a round head | White **bells** along one side of a stalk | Hooded green spathe with purple club |
| Toxicity | Edible | **DEADLY** (heart) | **Poisonous** (burning, swelling) |

## Also watch for
- [[s:bluebell]] leaves (narrow, keeled, slimy, no smell) and [[s:daffodil]] leaves (grey-green, thick, no smell).
- [[s:lesser-celandine]] (small, heart-shaped, glossy leaves) often grows in the same banks.

=== compare-cow-parsley
title: Cow parsley vs hemlock vs hogweed (and other white umbellifers)
summary: How to tell the common white umbrella-flowered plants apart, and why beginners should eat none of them.
tags: cow parsley, hemlock, hogweed, giant hogweed, umbellifer, apiaceae, carrot family, comparison, lookalike
compare: cow-parsley, hemlock, common-hogweed, giant-hogweed, hemlock-water-dropwort, fools-parsley
icon: flower-2
---
!quick
1. **Don't eat any white-umbrella (carrot family) plant** unless you are an expert. This family contains the UK's deadliest plants.
2. **Smooth stem with purple blotches + mousy smell = hemlock (deadly).**
3. **Huge (3–5 m) with blotched bristly stem = giant hogweed:** don't touch, the sap burns.
4. Waterside, celery smell, finger-like roots = **hemlock water-dropwort (deadly)**.
!end

## Side by side
| Feature | [[s:cow-parsley]] | [[s:hemlock]] | [[s:common-hogweed]] | [[s:giant-hogweed]] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Height | 0.6–1.5 m | 1.5–2.5 m | 1–2 m | **3–5 m** |
| Stem | Grooved, hollow, **slightly downy**, green (maybe purplish), **not spotted** | **Smooth, hairless, waxy, purple-blotched** | Ridged, hollow, **bristly-hairy** | Very thick, **purple-blotched and bristly** |
| Leaves | Fern-like, finely divided, soft, downy | Fern-like, finely divided, glossy, hairless | Broad, coarse, **lobed** and hairy | Enormous, jagged |
| Smell | Fresh, parsley-like | **Mousy, unpleasant** | Unpleasant, pig-like | Resinous |
| Flowers | **Apr–Jun** | **Jun–Jul** | May–Oct | Jun–Jul (umbels up to 80 cm) |
| Danger | Lookalikes are deadly | **DEADLY** | Sap burns skin in sunlight | **Severe sap burns** |

## Others to know
- [[s:hemlock-water-dropwort]]: wet places, broad wedge-shaped leaflets, celery smell, white finger-like tubers: **deadly**.
- [[s:fools-parsley]]: garden weed with long hanging bracts under each small umbel: poisonous.
- [[s:wild-celery]], [[s:fools-watercress]] and [[s:ground-elder]]: edible to experts, but easily confused.
- [[s:yarrow]] is **not** an umbellifer: its flat white heads are made of tiny daisies.

=== compare-field-mushroom
title: Field mushroom vs death cap and destroying angel
summary: The deadly white Amanitas compared with edible Agaricus mushrooms. Gill colour, volva and ring.
tags: field mushroom, death cap, destroying angel, amanita, agaricus, yellow stainer, comparison, lookalike, white mushroom
compare: field-mushroom, death-cap, destroying-angel, yellow-stainer, horse-mushroom
icon: mushroom
---
!quick
1. **Dig up the whole base.** A **bag-like sac (volva)** = Amanita = **never eat**.
2. **Gill colour:** mature field mushrooms have **pink → chocolate brown** gills. **White gills** in a mature mushroom = danger.
3. **Habitat:** field mushrooms grow in **grassland**; death caps and destroying angels grow under **trees**.
4. **Chrome-yellow stain** when the base is cut, inky smell = [[s:yellow-stainer]] (poisonous).
!end

## Side by side
| Feature | [[s:field-mushroom]] | [[s:death-cap]] | [[s:destroying-angel]] | [[s:yellow-stainer]] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cap | White to cream | **Olive-green to yellowish** (sometimes pale) | **Pure white** | White, often square-ish |
| Gills | **Pink**, then chocolate-brown | **White** | **White** | Pink-grey to brown |
| Spore print | Dark brown | White | White | Dark brown |
| Ring | Thin, fragile | Drooping skirt | Fragile, shaggy stem | Thick |
| Base | No volva | **Bag-like volva** | **Bag-like volva** | No volva; base stains **chrome yellow** |
| Where | Pasture, grass | Under oak, beech | Under birch, pine, beech | Woodland edges, gardens |
| Smell | Pleasant | Faint, sweet to sickly | Faint | **Ink / phenol** |
| Toxicity | Edible | **DEADLY** | **DEADLY** | Poisonous |

## Other traps
- Young Amanitas are white "eggs" like puffballs: cut every puffball in half ([[s:giant-puffball]], [[s:common-puffball]]).
- Shop-bought mushroom knowledge doesn't transfer safely to wild ones.

=== compare-chanterelle
title: Chanterelle vs false chanterelle vs jack o'lantern
summary: Tell the edible chanterelle from its orange lookalikes by its blunt ridges, white flesh and apricot smell.
tags: chanterelle, girolle, false chanterelle, jack o lantern, omphalotus, comparison, lookalike, orange mushroom
compare: chanterelle, false-chanterelle, jack-o-lantern, deadly-webcap
icon: mushroom
---
!quick
1. **Underside:** chanterelle has **blunt, shallow, forked ridges** running down the stem, not thin, sharp, crowded gills.
2. **Flesh:** chanterelle is **white to pale yellow** inside, firm and stringy. Orange flesh = not a chanterelle.
3. **Growth:** chanterelles grow **singly from soil**. Dense clusters on wood = jack o'lantern.
4. **Rusty-brown gills** = a webcap (potentially **deadly**).
!end

## Side by side
| Feature | [[s:chanterelle]] | [[s:false-chanterelle]] | [[s:jack-o-lantern]] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colour | Egg-yolk yellow to apricot | Bright orange, darker centre | Bright orange |
| Underside | **Blunt forked ridges** | Thin, crowded, forked **true gills** | Sharp, crowded **true gills** |
| Flesh | **White/pale**, firm, fibrous | Soft, orange-tinged | **Orange** |
| Smell | **Apricot** | Mushroomy | Strong, unpleasant |
| Growth | Singly or scattered, **on soil**, mossy woods | On conifer litter or rotting wood | **Clusters on wood** (oak, sweet chestnut) |
| Toxicity | Edible | Stomach upset | **Poisonous**: severe vomiting |

## Webcaps
- [[s:deadly-webcap]]: tawny-orange, pointed cap, rusty-brown gills, cobweb veil when young, in wet conifer woods. Causes kidney failure **days** later.

=== compare-elder-dogwood
title: Elder vs dogwood (and privet)
summary: Tell elderberries from bitter dogwood and poisonous privet berries by leaves, berry clusters and twigs.
tags: elder, elderberry, dogwood, privet, buckthorn, black berries, comparison, lookalike
compare: elder, dogwood, privet, buckthorn
icon: grape
---
!quick
1. **Elder leaves are divided** into 5–7 toothed leaflets. Dogwood and privet have **single, whole leaves**.
2. Elderberries: tiny (5 mm) in big, flat, **drooping** clusters on purple-red stalks.
3. Dogwood leaves have **veins curving to the tip**; its berries are held **upright**.
4. Even elderberries must be **cooked**.
!end

## Side by side
| Feature | [[s:elder]] | [[s:dogwood]] | [[s:privet]] | [[s:buckthorn]] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leaves | **Pinnate** (5–7 leaflets) | Simple, oval, smooth-edged; **veins curve to tip**; latex threads when torn | Small, simple, glossy, smooth-edged | Simple, finely toothed; veins curve to tip |
| Twigs | Corky bark, **white pith** | **Dark red** | Smooth, grey | Often ending in a thorn |
| Berry clusters | Large, flat, **drooping** heads of many tiny berries | Smaller upright clusters | Upright bunches, pea-sized | Clustered along twigs |
| Toxicity | Edible **cooked** | Poisonous (bitter) | Poisonous | Poisonous (violent purgative) |

=== compare-sloe-nightshade
title: Sloes vs deadly nightshade berries (and other black berries)
summary: How to tell blackthorn sloes and bilberries from deadly nightshade's glossy black berries.
tags: sloe, blackthorn, deadly nightshade, belladonna, bilberry, black berries, comparison, lookalike
compare: blackthorn, deadly-nightshade, bilberry, black-nightshade
icon: cherry
---
!quick
1. **Deadly nightshade:** single, **glossy**, cherry-sized black berries **sitting in a green 5-pointed star**, on a soft, leafy, non-woody plant. **Deadly.**
2. **Sloe:** blue-black with a **dusty white bloom**, on a **thorny woody** shrub with dark bark. Extremely sour.
3. **Bilberry:** small berries with a **crown scar**, on a knee-high shrub on **moorland**.
4. Unsure of any black berry → don't eat it.
!end

## Side by side
| Feature | [[s:blackthorn]] (sloe) | [[s:deadly-nightshade]] | [[s:bilberry]] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant | **Woody, thorny** shrub, black bark | Soft herb, 0.5–1.5 m, dies back in winter | Dwarf shrub, green ridged stems |
| Berry | 1–1.5 cm, **dusty bloom**, one stone | 1–1.5 cm, **glossy**, many tiny seeds | 5–9 mm, dull bloom, crown scar |
| Berry base | Short stalk | **Green 5-pointed star (calyx)** | Small stalk |
| Flowers | White, Mar–Apr, before leaves | Purple-brown **bells**, Jun–Aug | Pink-green bells, Apr–Jun |
| Where | Hedgerows | Woodland edges, chalk/limestone, ruins | Acid moorland, heaths |
| Toxicity | Edible (sour; don't eat stones) | **DEADLY** | Edible |

## Also
- [[s:black-nightshade]]: clusters of small dull black berries with white star flowers on a garden weed. Poisonous.
- [[s:privet]], [[s:dogwood]], [[s:buckthorn]] and [[s:ivy]] also have black berries: all poisonous.

=== compare-red-berries
title: Red berries: which are safe?

=== how-to-identify-plants
title: How to identify a plant properly
summary: A step-by-step method for identifying UK plants using leaves, stems, flowers, smell, habitat and season.
tags: identification, plants, botany, leaves, flowers, method, key
icon: sprout
---
!quick
1. Look at the **whole plant**: size, growth form (tree, shrub, herb, climber), habitat.
2. **Leaves:** simple or divided? Opposite or alternate? Edges, veins, hairs, smell when crushed.
3. **Stem:** round or square? Hollow? Hairy? Spotted? Sap colour?
4. **Flowers and fruit:** colour, petal count, arrangement. Match **every** feature, then check the lookalikes.
!end

## Key features
- **Leaf arrangement:** opposite pairs (nettle, elder) vs alternate (hazel, beech) vs whorls (cleavers) vs a rosette (dandelion).
- **Leaf type:** simple (one blade) vs compound (many leaflets: elder, rowan, hogweed).
- **Square stems** often mean the mint or dead-nettle family. **Hollow grooved stems with umbrella flowers** mean the carrot family: danger.
- **Smell** is strong evidence: garlic (wild garlic), mint, celery (beware water-dropwort), mousy (hemlock), almond (cherry laurel).
- **Season and habitat** narrow it down a lot.

## Good practice
- Compare against several sources and photos. Look at many plants, not one.
- If two features disagree, you have the wrong ID.

## Sources
- Botanical Society of Britain and Ireland (bsbi.org)
- Field Studies Council: Identification guides (field-studies-council.org)
- Plantlife: Wildflower identification

=== fungi-id-basics
title: Fungi identification basics (spore prints and safety)
summary: The features experts use to identify mushrooms, how to take a spore print, and the absolute safety rules.
tags: fungi, mushrooms, spore print, gills, volva, identification, safety
icon: mushroom
---
!quick
1. **Never eat a wild fungus** unless an expert has checked it in person. Delayed-symptom species kill.
2. **Collect the whole fungus**, including the base (twist and lift); note what it grew on and under which trees.
3. Check: cap, gills/pores/spines, gill attachment, ring, **volva**, stem, flesh colour change, smell, **spore print**.
4. **No Amanitas, no little brown mushrooms, no white-gilled woodland mushrooms.**
!end

## Taking a spore print
1. Cut off the cap and place it gills down on paper (half white, half dark), or on the steel pot lid.
2. Cover with a cup or bowl to stop drafts. Wait 2–12 hours.
3. Note the colour: **white** (Amanitas, many others), **pink** (wood blewit), **brown/rusty** (webcaps, funeral bell), **dark brown/purple-black** (Agaricus, sulphur tuft).

## High-risk groups
- **Amanita** (volva, ring, white gills): death cap, destroying angel, panther cap, fly agaric.
- **Webcaps (Cortinarius)**: rusty spores, cobweb veil.
- **Small brown mushrooms**: funeral bell and others with amatoxins.
- **Small white mushrooms in grass**: fool's funnel, deadly fibrecap.

=== berry-rules
title: Berry safety rules for the UK
summary: Simple, conservative rules for wild berries, including the beginner-safe "aggregate" berries.
tags: berries, rules, aggregate berries, blackberry, raspberry, safety
icon: grape
---
!quick
1. **Aggregate berries** made of many small round parts ([[s:blackberry]], [[s:raspberry]]) have no poisonous UK lookalikes.
2. Every other berry must be identified with certainty. Most UK hedgerow berries are poisonous.

=== bird-id-basics
title: UK bird identification basics
summary: Recognise common British birds by size, shape, behaviour, habitat and call. Useful for reading the land and the weather.
tags: birds, identification, crow, buzzard, robin, blackbird, gull, call, habitat

---
!quick
1. **Size and shape first:** compare with known birds (sparrow, blackbird, pigeon, crow).

=== tracks-signs
title: Animal tracks and signs
summary: Read the footprints, droppings, paths and feeding signs of common UK mammals.
tags: tracks, footprints, prints, droppings, scat, signs, deer, fox, badger, rabbit, tracking
icon: paw-print
---
!quick
1. **Deer:** two pointed "slots" (cloven hoof). Roe ~4.5 cm, red deer up to 9 cm.
2. **Fox:** oval print, 4 toes plus claws, in a **neat straight line**; musky smell.
3. **Badger:** broad pad with **5 toes in a line** and long claws; well-worn paths, setts with big spoil heaps.
4. **Rabbit:** long hind feet landing **in front** of the small fore feet; round droppings in piles.
!end

## More signs
- **Grey squirrel:** chewed hazelnuts split cleanly in half, and gnawed cones (cone "cores" with ragged scales).
- **Wild boar:** large areas of ploughed-up turf; muddy wallows.
- **Otter:** five-toed webbed prints by water; spraints (droppings) smelling of jasmine tea, on rocks.
- **Dog vs fox:** dog prints are rounder, with the outer toes splayed; fox prints are narrow and diamond-shaped.

## Uses
- Animal paths often lead to water. Tracks show fresh activity, and help you avoid boar or cattle.
