# UK weather. Met Office aligned.

=== uk-clouds
title: Reading UK clouds
summary: Recognise the clouds that bring rain, showers, storms and fair weather in Britain.
tags: clouds, cumulus, cumulonimbus, cirrus, stratus, nimbostratus, lenticular, forecast
wiki: Cumulonimbus cloud
icon: cloud
---
!quick
1. **Cirrus** (high, wispy "mares' tails") thickening into a milky sheet → a **warm front** and rain likely within **12–24 hours**.
2. **Towering cumulonimbus** (anvil top, dark base) → **heavy showers, hail, thunder** within the hour.
3. **Low grey stratus/nimbostratus** → steady rain or drizzle; hill fog.
4. **Small puffy cumulus** with blue sky → fair weather; they may grow into showers by afternoon.
!end

## The main clouds
| Cloud | Looks like | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Cirrus | High, thin wisps or hooks | Often the first sign of a front |
| Cirrostratus | Milky veil, **halo** round sun or moon | Rain likely within a day |
| Altostratus | Grey sheet; sun as if through frosted glass | Rain approaching soon |
| Nimbostratus | Thick, dark, featureless, low | Steady rain or snow |
| Stratus | Low grey layer, hill fog | Drizzle, poor visibility |
| Cumulus | White puffs, flat bases | Fair weather |
| Cumulonimbus | Huge towers, anvil top | Heavy showers, hail, lightning, squalls |
| Altocumulus ("mackerel sky") | Rows of small patches | Unsettled weather may follow |
| Lenticular | Smooth lens shapes over hills | Strong winds aloft; turbulence |

=== weather-fronts
title: Signs of an incoming front
summary: Read the sky, wind and pressure for the classic UK sequence of warm front rain followed by cold front showers.
tags: front, warm front, cold front, pressure, wind backing, veering, rain, forecast
icon: cloud-rain-wind
---
!quick
1. **Warm front coming:** cirrus → halo → thickening grey cloud → wind **backs** (turns anticlockwise, e.g. W → S) and strengthens → long steady rain.
2. **Warm sector:** mild, muggy, drizzle and hill fog.
3. **Cold front:** a line of dark cloud → sudden heavy rain and gusts → wind **veers** (clockwise, e.g. S → W/NW), temperature drops → clearer skies and showers.
4. **Falling pressure** (a barometer or phone app, if present) = worsening weather.
!end

## Notes
- Most UK weather arrives from the **west and south-west** on the jet stream.
- In the hills, the wind is much stronger and the temperature lower than in the valley (see [[a:mountain-weather]]).
- Folk lore has some basis: "Red sky at night" (clear skies to the west, where weather comes from) is roughly right in Britain; "mackerel sky and mares' tails make tall ships carry low sails" refers to an approaching front.

=== mountain-weather
title: Mountain weather in Britain
summary: Why hills are colder, windier and wetter than the forecast for the valleys, and how fast it changes.
tags: mountain weather, hills, wind, temperature, lapse rate, hill fog, summit
icon: mountain-snow
---
!quick
1. Temperature drops roughly **1 °C for every 150 m** you climb (more in dry air).
2. Wind on summits and ridges is often **2–3 times** stronger than in the valley; gusts are worse.
3. Hill fog can drop in minutes. Know your route off.
4. Turn back early; the mountain will be there another day.
!end

## Notes
- A mild 12 °C wet day at sea level can be 4 °C with a 40 mph wind on a 1,000 m summit: a wind chill well below freezing.
- Snow lingers on north-facing slopes and in gullies into early summer in Scotland.

=== wind-chill
title: Wind chill
summary: How wind makes cold air feel much colder and speeds up hypothermia and frostbite.
tags: wind chill, feels like, cold, wind, temperature, exposure
icon: wind
---
!quick
1. Wind strips away the warm air layer around you: **windproof** layers matter as much as warm ones.
2. **Wet + wind** is far worse than dry cold.
3. Get out of the wind first: behind a wall, boulder or bank, or into trees.
!end

## Approximate "feels like" temperatures
| Air temp | 10 mph wind | 20 mph | 30 mph | 40 mph |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 °C | 8 | 6 | 5 | 4 |
| 5 °C | 2 | 0 | −1 | −2 |
| 0 °C | −4 | −6 | −8 | −9 |
| −5 °C | −10 | −13 | −14 | −16 |
Values are approximate (Met Office "feels like" style), and wet skin and clothing make it worse.

=== lightning-safety
title: Lightning safety
summary: When to take cover from thunderstorms, where not to be, and what to do if caught in the open.
tags: lightning, thunder, storm, thunderstorm, 30 30 rule, safety
icon: cloud-lightning
---
!quick
1. **Hear thunder? You're in range.** Count from flash to thunder: under **30 seconds** (about 10 km) → take shelter.
2. Get **off summits, ridges and open ground**; away from lone trees, fences, metal and water.
3. Best shelter: a building or a car. Woods with trees of similar height are better than open ground.
4. Wait **30 minutes** after the last thunder.
!end

## Caught in the open
- Move to lower ground; spread a group out (at least 5–10 m apart) so one strike can't hurt everyone.
- Crouch low with feet together on an insulating mat or rucksack, minimising contact with the ground. Don't lie flat.
- Put down trekking poles and anything metal.

## If someone is struck
- They don't hold a charge: it is safe to touch them. Check breathing and start CPR if needed ([[a:cpr-adult]]). Send for help.

=== cold-and-wet
title: Cold and wet: the real UK killer
summary: Why hypothermia happens in mild British weather, and the habits that prevent it.
tags: cold, wet, rain, wind, hypothermia, cotton, clothing, exposure
icon: umbrella
---
!quick
1. **Stay dry** inside: vent before you sweat; put the waterproof on **before** you get wet.
2. **Block the wind.**
3. **Eat and drink** regularly: you can't stay warm on an empty tank.
4. **Act early** on shivering and clumsiness: stop, shelter, add layers ([[a:hypothermia]]).
!end

## Why the UK is dangerous
- Typical hill weather: 3–10 °C, rain and a strong wind. Wet clothing loses most of its insulating power, and wind steals heat by convection.
- Fatigue, hunger and dehydration reduce heat production. Many mountain rescue casualties are simply wet, cold and exhausted.
- **Cotton** (jeans, T-shirts, hoodies) soaks up water, dries very slowly and chills you. Wool and synthetics keep some warmth when wet.

## Habits
- Keep a dry warm layer, a hat and gloves sealed in a bag.
- Change wet base layers at camp, and put on dry sleeping clothes.
- Shelter early, rather than pushing on.

=== layering
title: Dressing in layers
summary: The base, mid and shell layer system: stay warm and dry by adding and removing layers as you work and rest.
tags: layers, clothing, base layer, fleece, waterproof, insulation, cotton
icon: shirt
---
!quick
1. **Base layer:** wool or synthetic next to the skin, never cotton.
2. **Mid layer:** fleece or wool for warmth; an insulated jacket for stops.
3. **Shell:** waterproof and windproof jacket and trousers.
4. **Be bold, start cold:** start walking slightly cool; add layers at stops **before** you chill.
!end

## Notes
- Lose a layer as soon as you start to sweat; sweat-soaked layers chill you when you stop.
- A hat, buff and gloves make a huge difference for little weight.
- Keep a spare dry layer and socks in a waterproof bag.

=== heat-and-sun
title: Heat and sun in the UK
summary: Sunburn, heat illness and dehydration happen in Britain too, especially in late spring and summer.
tags: sun, heat, UV, sunburn, summer, hot weather
icon: sun-medium
---
!quick
1. UV is strongest **11:00–15:00** from **March to October**, even when cloudy.
2. Cover up: hat, long sleeves; shade in the middle of the day.
3. Drink regularly; watch for [[a:heat-illness]].
!end

## Notes
- Sun reflected off water, snow and pale rock increases exposure.
- Sunburn damages your ability to sweat and cool down.

=== weather-lore
title: Weather signs without a forecast
summary: Natural signs that help predict the next few hours of weather when you have no forecast.
tags: weather lore, red sky, signs, forecast, birds, dew, smoke
icon: sunset
---
!quick
1. **Clear night with heavy dew or frost** → fine morning; rain is unlikely that night.
2. **Halo round the sun or moon** → rain within a day.
3. **Wind backing and freshening, cloud lowering** → rain coming.
4. **Smoke that hangs low and drifts** → falling pressure, unsettled weather.
!end

## Notes
- "Red sky at night, shepherd's delight; red sky in the morning, shepherd's warning" works reasonably well in the UK because weather tends to arrive from the west.
- Distant sounds and smells carrying unusually clearly often come before rain.
- None of this is a forecast: be ready for change.
