!quick
1. **Never eat any root, stem or leaf of a carrot-family (umbrella-flowered) plant growing in or near water.** No exceptions for beginners.
2. **Cluster of pale, fat, finger-like roots + celery or parsley smell + wet ground = hemlock water-dropwort.** A small piece of root can kill.
3. **Someone has eaten it?** Send for help at once, even if they feel fine. Seizures can start within an hour.
4. Keep a sample of the plant and note the time. If they fit, protect them from injury; if they stop breathing normally, start CPR.
!end

!danger Why this plant kills foragers
[[s:hemlock-water-dropwort]] is probably the most poisonous plant in Britain. Its toxin, oenanthotoxin, causes violent seizures and can stop breathing within hours. The roots are the most poisonous part, but leaves and stems are dangerous too. It **smells pleasant**, like celery or parsley, and its roots look and even taste like parsnip, so nothing warns you. People in Britain and Ireland have died after mistaking it for wild parsnip, wild celery or water parsnip. Livestock and dogs die after eating roots washed out by floods or dug up during ditch clearing.
!end

## The rule
Most carrot-family plants that grow in wet ground are poisonous, several deadly, and the edible ones look like them. Even experienced foragers have been caught out. **If it has umbrella-shaped heads of small flowers (or leaves like celery, parsley or carrot) and it grows beside a stream, river, ditch, canal, pond or marsh, don't eat it.** That includes the roots, the leaves and the stems, at any time of year.

There is plenty of safer wild food in Britain. See [[a:foraging-rules]].

## How to recognise hemlock water-dropwort
Several features together, never one:
- **Place:** in or right beside water, often in dense clumps along the bank. Exposed roots are common where the bank has eroded.
- **Season:** fresh green leaves appear in **late autumn** and stay through **winter and spring**, just when there is little else green and foragers go looking for greens.
- **Leaves:** glossy, mid to bright green, triangular in outline, divided three or four times into broad, wedge-shaped leaflets with toothed tips. Think flat-leaf parsley or celery leaves, but bigger.
- **Roots:** cut or dig nothing up to check, but if roots are exposed you will see a bundle of 3–10 or more pale, swollen tubers, each up to finger or thumb size, hanging from the stem base.
- **Sap:** a cut stem or root weeps watery sap that turns yellow, then orange, after a few minutes in the air. Wash your hands after touching it, and never taste it to "check".
- **Flowers:** large, domed umbels of white flowers in June and July, on hollow, grooved stems.

## Other waterside umbellifers
- [[s:fools-watercress]]: creeps and roots in streams and ditches, often growing among watercress, with once-divided leaves of toothed, pointed leaflets and tiny stalkless white umbels. It is edible itself, but young hemlock water-dropwort grows in the same places and looks alike. Leave it.
- **Water parsnips** (greater and lesser water-parsnip): once-divided leaves of toothed oval leaflets, white umbels, in shallow water and fens. Not safe to eat, and too like the deadly species.
- [[s:hemlock]]: also loves damp ditches and riverbanks. Smooth stems with **purple blotches**, lacy leaves, a **mousy** smell. Deadly. See [[a:compare-cow-parsley]].
- [[s:cowbane]]: rare, in shallow water and marshes in parts of Britain; deadly. Its rootstock, cut lengthways, shows hollow chambers.
- [[s:common-hogweed]] and [[s:cow-parsley]]: more often on verges and woodland edges than in water, but both turn up on banks. Neither is worth the risk.
- [[s:watercress]] is **not** an umbellifer: it has small **four-petalled** white flowers in loose spikes and a peppery taste. Hemlock water-dropwort and fool's watercress often grow tangled in it, so check every stem you pick, and cook it (watercress from wild streams can carry liver fluke).

## If someone may have eaten it
Symptoms usually start within **1–2 hours**, sometimes much sooner:
- Burning mouth, drooling, nausea, vomiting and stomach pain.
- Dilated pupils, sweating, a flushed face, confusion.
- **Seizures**, which can come one after another, then difficulty breathing and collapse.

What to do:
1. **Send for help now**, even if they feel well. Two people go, with a written location report. Tell whoever raises the alarm "possible hemlock water-dropwort poisoning".
2. **Don't** make them vomit. Rinse the mouth; nothing more than sips of water.
3. **Keep a sample**: the plant, any leftovers and vomit in a bag or cloth, and photograph it. Note what was eaten, how much and when.
4. **Seizures:** move hard objects away, cushion the head, don't hold them down or put anything in the mouth, and time it. Afterwards, put them in the [[a:recovery-position]].
5. Unresponsive and not breathing normally: start [[a:cpr-adult]].
