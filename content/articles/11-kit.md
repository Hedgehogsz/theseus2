# My kit: exactly what this device supports, plus suggestions.

=== kit-checklist
title: Kit checklist
summary: Your exact survival kit, with tick boxes to check before you go, and suggested additions.
tags: kit, checklist, gear, packing list, equipment
icon: list-checks
---
!quick
1. Tick every item below before you leave. The ticks are saved.
2. **Fire:** ferro rod, flint and steel with char cloth tin, magnifier, arc lighter (charged).
3. **Water:** stainless steel pot, single-walled steel bottle, cotton straining cloth.
4. **Power:** 20 W solar panel and cable; this phone charged, in a waterproof bag.
!end

!checklist Your kit
- Ferro rod (½″ × 6″) and striker: [[a:kit-ferro-rod]]
- Flint and high-carbon steel striker: [[a:kit-flint-steel]]
- Char cloth in an airtight tin (and spare cotton to make more): [[a:char-cloth]]
- Glass magnifier in a protective case: [[a:kit-magnifier]]
- Rechargeable arc lighter, charged: [[a:arc-lighter]]
- Stainless steel pot (with lid if possible): [[a:kit-pot-bottle]]
- Single-walled stainless steel bottle: [[a:kit-pot-bottle]]
- Cotton cloth for straining water: [[a:kit-cotton-cloth]]
- 20 W USB solar panel and cable: [[a:solar-panel]]
- This phone (the guide), charged, in a sealed bag: [[a:kit-phone]]
!end

!checklist Strongly recommended additions
- Whistle (on a cord, in an outside pocket)
- Map (OS 1:25 000 or 1:50 000) and baseplate compass, in a waterproof case
- Headtorch with spare batteries
- Survival bag (orange plastic) or group shelter
- Small first-aid kit: plasters, dressings, tape, tick remover, painkillers, any personal medicines
- Knife (sheath or folding: see [[a:knife-safety]])
- 10–20 m of strong cord, and a small tarp
- Spare warm layer, hat and gloves in a dry bag; waterproof jacket and trousers
- Emergency food (high energy), and a written emergency contact card
!end

## Before you go
- Tell someone your route and when you'll be back.
- Check the weather forecast, if you can get one.
- Charge the phone and arc lighter; dry the char cloth tin.

=== kit-ferro-rod
title: Your ferro rod: use and care
summary: Care and handling for your ½″ × 6″ ferrocerium rod, the most reliable fire starter in your kit.
tags: ferro rod, ferrocerium, care, maintenance, kit
icon: sparkles
---
!quick
1. Technique: [[a:ferro-rod]]. Hold the striker still and **pull the rod back**.
2. Use the **full length** evenly; firm pressure.
3. Keep it **dry**: ferrocerium oxidises in damp air. A quick scrape restores it.
4. Store the striker with the rod so you don't lose it; the spine of a knife also works if it has a sharp 90° edge.
!end

## Lifetime
A ½″ × 6″ rod is good for thousands of fires. It works wet: shake it off and strike.

=== kit-flint-steel
title: Your flint and steel: use and care
summary: Look after your high-carbon striker, find good flint, and keep char cloth dry.
tags: flint, steel, striker, char cloth, care, kit
icon: sparkle
---
!quick
1. Technique: [[a:flint-and-steel]]. Sparks only land well on **char cloth** or similar.
2. Keep the striker **dry and lightly oiled**; rust kills sparks.
3. **Flint:** pick up sharp-edged pieces in chalk country (fields, beaches); quartz or chert elsewhere.
4. Make char cloth in batches: [[a:char-cloth]].
!end

## Notes
- A dull flint edge stops sparking: knock off a flake to expose a fresh edge (away from your eyes).
- Carry the whole set together in the char cloth tin.

=== kit-magnifier
title: Your magnifier: use and care
summary: Fuel-free fire on sunny days, plus close-up identification of plants and fungi.
tags: magnifier, lens, sun fire, identification, care, kit
icon: zoom-in
---
!quick
1. Sun fire: [[a:magnifier-fire]]. Clear sun, still hands, dark tinder.
2. Also use it to **examine plants and fungi**: hairs, gill attachment, tiny bracts (e.g. [[s:fools-parsley]]).
3. **Never** look at the sun through it, and never leave it in sunlight.
4. Keep it in a case or wrapped in cloth; clean with a soft cloth.
!end

=== kit-pot-bottle
title: Your steel pot and bottle: use and care
summary: Your water purification system: boiling safely, cleaning, and avoiding damage.
tags: pot, bottle, stainless steel, boil, care, kit
icon: cooking-pot
---
!quick
1. **Boil water:** [[a:settle-strain-boil]] and [[a:boiling-steel-bottle-pot]].
2. **Never heat a sealed or insulated bottle.** Cap off, single-wall only.
3. Clean with sand, [[s:horsetail]] or a cloth; rinse with boiled water.
4. Keep "dirty" (untreated) and "clean" (boiled) use separate where possible.
!end

## Notes
- Soot on the outside actually helps the pot absorb heat. Keep the inside clean.
- A pot lid saves fuel and boils water faster. Improvise one from a flat stone or foil.
- The pot lid or a polished bottle can be a signal mirror ([[a:signalling-rescue]]).

=== kit-cotton-cloth
title: Your cotton cloth: use and care
summary: Strain water, make char cloth from spares, and keep the straining cloth clean.
tags: cotton, cloth, strain, filter, char cloth, kit
icon: filter
---
!quick
1. **Strain** water through 2–4 folded layers before boiling ([[a:settle-strain-boil]]).
2. Rinse it in **boiled** water after use and dry it in the sun if possible.
3. Keep a **separate** piece for char cloth, wound padding or slings.
!end

## Notes
- Cotton is a poor clothing layer when wet ([[a:cold-and-wet]]), but excellent for straining and charring.
- Sunlight helps dry and sanitise the cloth; it is not enough to make water safe.

=== solar-panel
title: Your 20 W solar panel
summary: Get the most from a 20 W USB panel in British light: angle, direct sun, heat, and what to charge.
tags: solar panel, solar charging, USB, 20W, sun, power, charge phone
icon: solar-panel
---
!quick
1. Needs **direct sun**. Point the panel **square to the sun**; re-angle every hour.
2. Keep the **phone in the shade** (under the panel or in a bag) with a longer cable; hot phones charge slowly and age fast.
3. Charge the **arc lighter** and **phone** in the middle of sunny days. Overcast UK days may give almost nothing.
4. Clouds interrupt charging; this is normal (many panels auto-restart).
!end

## What to expect in the UK
- In strong summer sun a 20 W panel may deliver 5–10 W to a phone. That can mean several hours for a full charge.
- In winter, or under cloud, output may be a small fraction of that.
- Charge whenever the sun is out, not when the battery is already empty.

## Care
- Keep the surface clean and dry; don't fold it while wet. Avoid sharp creases.
- Protect the USB socket from rain; dry it before use.

=== kit-phone
title: This phone: care and limits
summary: Keep the guide phone charged, dry and ready, and know what it can't do: it has no SIM and cannot call for help.
tags: phone, battery, waterproof, kit, Samsung, torch, radio, no SIM
icon: smartphone
---
!quick
1. **It cannot call 999 or 112.** It has no SIM card, and UK networks don't connect SIM-less emergency calls. Use [[a:signalling-rescue|signals]] and [[a:finding-help|people]] instead.
2. Keep it charged (top up monthly), **off** between uses, and in a **sealed waterproof bag**.
3. Use its **torch** for night signalling (six flashes a minute) and its screen or case as a signal mirror.
4. Tools screen: torch, FM radio (needs wired headphones as an aerial), camera, calculator, clock.
!end

## What it is good for
- The whole guide works offline, with no network and no SIM.
- **Location report:** fill it in and show the screen to whoever goes for help or reaches you ([[a:location-report]]).
- **FM radio:** BBC local radio and national stations carry weather warnings and news of major incidents.
- **Camera:** photograph a plant or fungus to compare with the guide, or photograph your map to save handling it in the rain.

## Care
- The A21s is **not waterproof**: keep it bagged outdoors, and dry it fully before charging if it gets wet.
- Cold drains the battery fast; keep it in an inside pocket ([[a:batteries-electronics]]).
- Kiosk mode: to leave the guide, long-press the title for 5 seconds (hidden menu).
